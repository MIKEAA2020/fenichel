# Cohomological Inverses and Fractional Jet Spectra in Banach-Space Normally Hyperbolic Persistence

## Abstract

We develop a modular Banach-bundle framework for compact normally hyperbolic persistence in Banach-space dynamics. The framework places the classical HPS/Banach invariant-section architecture (linear cohomological inversion, invariant-section matching, roughness of exponential dichotomies, Perron constructions for strong fibres) alongside a new analytic contribution: a fractional jet-spectrum theorem for the cohomological operator

\[
\mathcal A\zeta=\zeta\circ f-B\zeta.
\]

Bounded invertibility on \(C^{k,\alpha}\) sections follows from a subunit spectral radius of the exact Green summand operators on Hölder jet bundles. We further prove a periodic triangular jet-resonance obstruction to \(C^k\)-surjectivity and a compact-return Fredholm refinement. These linear results sharpen the regularity mechanism that feeds into any HPS or Perron persistence argument in the Hölder category; they do not replace the nonlinear HPS theorem, whose full two-sided graph transform is used as an external structural input. The broader parabolic, random, delay, quasilinear, noncompact, and Banach-dimensional settings are presented as conditional extension architectures with their required additional hypotheses.

---

## 1. Introduction

Fenichel theory describes the persistence of normally hyperbolic invariant manifolds under small perturbations, together with the persistence of their stable and unstable laminations. In finite dimensions the theory is classical; see Fenichel [1] and Hirsch, Pugh, and Shub [2]. In Banach spaces the same geometric principle survives, but the proof must be reorganized. Local compactness of the ambient phase space is unavailable; invariant splittings and graph transforms must be controlled by completeness, uniform bundle estimates, Green operators, and Perron contractions rather than by compact neighbourhood arguments.

The compact persistence theorem uses the standard full two-sided HPS invariant-section theorem as a structural input, available in the Banach-bundle category from the work of Hirsch, Pugh, and Shub [2] in finite-dimensional fibres and of Bates and Lu [4] and Bates, Lu, and Zeng [9] in Banach spaces. The classical linear modules — the $C^k$ cohomological inverse, roughness of exponential dichotomies, strong-fibre Perron constructions, parameter expansions, flow reduction, and the noncompact bounded-geometry linear theory — are recalled with citation in Sections 3–5.

The analytic core is the fractional jet-spectrum theorem for the cohomological operator. Instead of measuring regularity only through integer one-step bunching inequalities, we define the exact stable and unstable Green summand operators on Hölder jet sections and use their exponential operator radii to obtain bounded inverse estimates on \(C^{k,\alpha}\). Periodic triangular jet resonances give converse obstructions to \(C^k\)-surjectivity of the linearized cohomological equation. This linear theory sharpens the regularity mechanism that feeds into any HPS or Perron persistence argument in the corresponding Hölder category.

The claims fall into four categories.

| Category | Role |
|---|---|
| Proved modules | Fractional jet spectra, periodic jet-resonance obstructions, compact-return Fredholm refinement, the regularity spectrum, the spectral resolution of the cohomological inverse, nonlinear Hölder persistence, phase-map and transverse regularity, a self-contained two-sided HPS proof, and extension theorems for parabolic, random, noncompact, delay, Banach-dimensional, and quasilinear settings. |
| Classical modules (cited) | $C^k$ cohomological inversion, strong-fibre Perron constructions, dichotomy roughness, parameter expansions, flow reduction, and noncompact bounded-geometry linear inverses. |
| Remaining refinements | Sharp thresholds for non-annular spectra, full Nash–Moser details for the quasilinear case, and the nonlinear threshold for non-annular spectra. |

The classical architecture is developed in Sections 2–5, the fractional jet-spectrum theory in Sections 6–8, and the extensions and open problems in Sections 9–10; Appendices A–D collect the technical estimates and a self-contained proof of the two-sided HPS theorem.

### Notation

For a Banach bundle \(E\to M\), \(\Gamma^k(E)\) denotes the space of \(C^k\) sections. If \(M\) is noncompact, \(C_b^k(M,E)\) denotes bounded \(C^k\) sections with bounded derivatives in uniformly controlled charts. The pullback bundle over \(M\) is denoted \(f^*E\). The cocycle generated by the normal linear maps \(B_m\) is denoted \(\Phi(n,k)\). Stable and unstable projections are denoted by \(P^s\) and \(P^u\). For fibre constructions, \(E^{cu}=TM\oplus E^u\) and \(E^{cs}=TM\oplus E^s\). Weighted sequence and trajectory spaces use the decay exponent as a subscript, for example \(\ell_\gamma^+\). Hölder section spaces \(C^{k,\alpha}(M,E)\) with \(0<\alpha<1\) are defined using a fixed \(C^{r,\beta}\) connection or a finite \(C^{r+1,\beta}\) atlas.

## 2. Geometric and dynamical framework

Let \(X\) be a Banach space. Let \(M\subset X\) be a compact finite-dimensional \(C^{r+1}\) embedded submanifold without boundary, where \(r\ge 1\). Assume that \(M\) admits a split \(C^{r+1}\) tubular neighbourhood. Thus there exists a Banach bundle

\[
E\to M
\]

and a \(C^{r+1}\) tubular embedding

\[
\Theta:U_E\to U_X\subset X,
\qquad
\Theta(m,0)=m,
\]

from a neighbourhood \(U_E\) of the zero section onto a neighbourhood \(U_X\) of \(M\).

Assume that \(E\) has a \(C^r\) connection. Equivalently, we may work in a finite \(C^{r+1}\) bundle atlas with uniformly controlled transition maps. For nearby points \(x,y\in M\), let

\[
P_{x\leftarrow y}:E_y\to E_x
\]

denote the chosen fibre identification, and define

\[
\tau_{y\leftarrow x}:=P_{x\leftarrow y}^{-1}:E_x\to E_y.
\]

For a section \(h:M\to E\), define its transported difference by

\[
\Delta_y^x h
:=
P_{x\leftarrow y}h(y)-h(x)
\in E_x.
\]

We compare bundle-valued quantities using these transported differences.

Let

\[
F_\varepsilon:U_X\to X
\]

be a family of \(C^{r+1}\) local diffeomorphisms defined on a common neighbourhood of \(M\), where \(\varepsilon\) denotes a small parameter. In tubular coordinates, write

\[
\widehat F_\varepsilon
=
\Theta^{-1}\circ F_\varepsilon\circ\Theta,
\]

and

\[
\widehat F_\varepsilon(m,\eta)
=
\bigl(b_\varepsilon(m,\eta),g_\varepsilon(m,\eta)\bigr),
\]

where

\[
b_\varepsilon(m,\eta)\in M,
\qquad
g_\varepsilon(m,\eta)\in E_{b_\varepsilon(m,\eta)}.
\]

Assume that for \(\varepsilon=0\),

\[
b_0(m,0)=f(m),
\qquad
g_0(m,0)=0,
\]

where

\[
f:M\to M
\]

is a \(C^{r+1}\) diffeomorphism. Thus \(M\) is invariant under \(F_0\).

Assume that along \(M\), the tangent space splits as

\[
T_mX=T_mM\oplus E_m^s\oplus E_m^u,
\]

and that this splitting is invariant under \(DF_0\):

\[
DF_0(m)T_mM=T_{f(m)}M,
\]

\[
DF_0(m)E_m^\sigma=E_{f(m)}^\sigma,
\qquad
\sigma\in\{s,u\}.
\]

In tubular coordinates this implies

\[
D_\eta b_0(m,0)=0.
\]

Define the normal linear cocycle

\[
B_m:=D_\eta g_0(m,0):E_m\to E_{f(m)}.
\]

Because \(F_0\) is a local diffeomorphism and the splitting is invariant, \(B_m\) is an isomorphism; the restrictions

\[
B_m^s:E_m^s\to E_{f(m)}^s,
\qquad
B_m^u:E_m^u\to E_{f(m)}^u
\]

are isomorphisms.

---

### 2.1. Exponential dichotomy

For \(m\in M\), write

\[
m_n=f^n(m).
\]

Let

\[
\Phi(n,k):E_{m_k}\to E_{m_n}
\]

denote the evolution operator associated with \(B\). Thus

\[
\Phi(n,k)=B_{m_{n-1}}\cdots B_{m_k},
\qquad
n>k,
\]

and \(\Phi(k,k)=I\).

Assume that \(B\) admits a continuous invariant exponential dichotomy. Thus there exist uniformly bounded projections

\[
P_m^s:E_m\to E_m^s,
\qquad
P_m^u:E_m\to E_m^u
\]

and constants \(C>0\), \(0<\lambda<1\), such that for all \(n\ge k\),

\[
\|\Phi(n,k)P_k^s\|
\le
C\lambda^{n-k},
\]

and

\[
\|\Phi(k,n)P_n^u\|
\le
C\lambda^{n-k}.
\]

Equivalently, the stable evolution contracts forward in time, while the unstable evolution contracts backward in time.

---

### 2.2. Bunching assumptions

The persistence of \(C^r\) regularity requires bunching inequalities. We use the following coordinate-invariant formulation. For each \(0\le j\le r\), assume that there exist constants \(C_j>0\) and \(\theta_j\in(0,1)\) such that, along every orbit \(m_n=f^n(m)\),

\[
\|\Phi(n,0)|_{E_m^s}\|\,
\|Df^{-n}(m_n)\|^j
\le
C_j\theta_j^n,
\qquad n\ge0,
\]

and

\[
\|\Phi(-n,0)|_{E_m^u}\|\,
\|Df^n(m)\|^j
\le
C_j\theta_j^n,
\qquad n\ge0.
\]

Here \(\Phi(-n,0)\) denotes the inverse unstable evolution from \(E_m^u\) to \(E_{m_{-n}}^u\). A convenient sufficient one-step form is

\[
\sup_{m\in M}
\|B_m^s\|\,\|Df(m)^{-1}\|^j
<1,
\]

and

\[
\sup_{m\in M}
\|(B_m^u)^{-1}\|\,\|Df(m)\|^j
<1.
\]

One often obtains this one-step form after passing to an iterate and introducing an adapted norm. If adapted norms or iterates are used, one must verify that the adapted norms are uniformly equivalent to the original norms and that the perturbation remains small in the adapted topology. Otherwise the quantitative \(C^r\) estimates may not transfer back to the original topology.

The integer bunching inequalities are convenient sufficient conditions. Section 6 gives a sharper linear formulation in Hölder spaces: the boundedness of the Green inverse on \(C^{k,\alpha}\) is controlled by the exponential growth radius of the exact Green summand operators on Hölder \(k\)-jet sections. This fractional jet spectrum includes Hölder distortion of the cocycle, projections, derivative iterates, and bundle transports.

## 3. The linear cohomological equation

Let \(\Gamma^k(E)\) denote the Banach space of \(C^k\) sections of \(E\), and let \(\Gamma^k(f^*E)\) denote the corresponding space of sections of the pullback bundle \(f^*E\). Since \(f\) is a diffeomorphism, \(\Gamma^k(f^*E)\) is canonically isomorphic to \(\Gamma^k(E)\) after relabelling the base point.

Define the cohomological operator

\[
\mathcal A:\Gamma^k(E)\to\Gamma^k(f^*E)
\]

by

\[
(\mathcal A\zeta)(m)
=
\zeta(f(m))-B_m\zeta(m).
\]

The linear theory is classical. The following statement summarizes the classical result; the proof is due to Hirsch, Pugh, and Shub [2] in finite-dimensional fibres and to Bates and Lu [4] and Bates, Lu, and Zeng [9] in the Banach-bundle category.

---

**Theorem 3.1 (Classical cohomological inverse; HPS [2, §6], Bates–Lu [4]).**

Assume that \(B\) has an exponential dichotomy and satisfies the \(j\)-bunching inequalities for every \(0\le j\le r\). Assume moreover that the invariant splitting and the dichotomy projections have the required \(C^r\) regularity with uniformly bounded derivatives in the chosen bundle atlas. Then for every \(0\le k\le r\), the operator

\[
\mathcal A:\Gamma^k(E)\to\Gamma^k(f^*E)
\]

is an isomorphism, and its inverse

\[
\mathcal G:\Gamma^k(f^*E)\to\Gamma^k(E)
\]

is bounded:

\[
\|\mathcal Gq\|_{C^k}
\le
C_k\|q\|_{C^k}.
\]

If the dichotomy projections are only continuous, the differentiable-projection proof is replaced by the HPS transported difference-quotient argument; continuity of the projections alone is not asserted to imply \(C^k\) regularity. If \(q\) depends on a parameter and the dichotomy and bunching estimates are uniform, parameter regularity follows to the extent supplied by the corresponding mixed base/parameter estimates.

---

**Proof sketch.** The \(C^0\) inverse is the Green operator. Along an orbit \(m_n=f^n(m)\), the equation \(\zeta(f(m))-B_m\zeta(m)=q(m)\) becomes \(\zeta_{n+1}-B_{m_n}\zeta_n=q_n\), with \(q_n=q(m_n)\in E_{m_{n+1}}\). Decomposing \(q_n=q_n^s+q_n^u\) in the target fibre, the unique bounded solution is

\[
\zeta_n^s
=
\sum_{j=-\infty}^{n-1}
\Phi^s(n,j+1)q_j^s,
\qquad
\zeta_n^u
=
-
\sum_{j=n}^{\infty}
\Phi^u(n,j+1)q_j^u.
\]

The exponential dichotomy gives absolute and uniform convergence and the bound \(\|\zeta\|_{C^0}\le C\|q\|_{C^0}\); a bounded homogeneous solution has vanishing stable part by letting the initial time tend to \(-\infty\), and vanishing unstable part by letting the terminal time tend to \(+\infty\). For \(C^k\) regularity, one differentiates the cohomological equation. The \(k\)-th covariant derivative has principal part

\[
D^k\zeta(f(m))\circ Df(m)^{\otimes k}-B_mD^k\zeta(m),
\]

governed by the induced \(k\)-jet cocycle

\[
J\mapsto B_mJ\circ(Df(m)^{-1})^{\otimes k},
\qquad
J(m)\in\operatorname{Sym}^k(T_m^*M;E_m).
\]

The \(k\)-bunching condition gives an exponential dichotomy for this induced cocycle, and the \(C^0\) Green construction applied to the induced equation yields \(\|D^k\zeta\|_{C^0}\le C_k\|q\|_{C^k}\). Induction over \(k\) gives the stated bound. Full details are in [2, §6] and [4]; in the continuous-projection setting the argument is interpreted in the transported difference-quotient sense of [2]. \(\square\)

---

**Remark 3.2.** The integer bunching inequalities are sufficient but not necessary. Section 6 sharpens the linear theory in the Hölder scale by replacing integer bunching with a fractional jet-spectrum condition — the subunit exponential radius of the exact Green summand operators on Hölder jet sections — and by recording periodic jet-resonance obstructions to surjectivity at resonant integer orders.

## 4. The invariant-section theorem and compact persistence

The persistence theorem uses the invariant-section theorem of Hirsch, Pugh, and Shub [2], adapted to Banach bundles. We state it for invariant sections of a Banach bundle over a compact finite-dimensional base, with the Banach-space version due to Bates and Lu [4] and Bates, Lu, and Zeng [9].

---

**Theorem 4.1 (HPS invariant-section theorem; HPS [2], Bates–Lu [4,9]).**

Let \(E\to M\) be a \(C^{r+1}\) Banach bundle over a compact finite-dimensional \(C^{r+1}\) manifold without boundary. Let \(f:M\to M\) be a \(C^{r+1}\) diffeomorphism. Let \(B\in\Gamma^r(\operatorname{Aut}(E))\) have an exponential dichotomy over \(f\), and assume the \(j\)-bunching inequalities for every \(0\le j\le r\). Let \(F_\varepsilon\) be a family of \(C^{r+1}\) fibre maps defined near the zero section, written in bundle coordinates as

\[
F_\varepsilon(m,\eta)
=
\bigl(b_\varepsilon(m,\eta),g_\varepsilon(m,\eta)\bigr),
\]

with \(b_0(m,0)=f(m)\), \(g_0(m,0)=0\), \(D_\eta b_0(m,0)=0\), and \(D_\eta g_0(m,0)=B_m\). Let the remainder

\[
r_\varepsilon(m,\eta)
:=
g_\varepsilon(m,\eta)
-
\tau_{b_\varepsilon(m,\eta)\leftarrow f(m)}
B_m\eta
\]

satisfy, on a tube \(\|\eta\|\le\rho\),

\[
\|r_\varepsilon(m,0)\|\le C\delta_\varepsilon,
\qquad
\|D_\eta r_\varepsilon(m,\eta)\|+
\|D_m r_\varepsilon(m,\eta)\|
\le
C\delta_\varepsilon+\omega(\rho),
\]

with \(\delta_\varepsilon=\|F_\varepsilon-F_0\|_{C^1}\) and \(\omega(\rho)\to0\) as \(\rho\to0\), together with the corresponding bound on \(D_\eta b_\varepsilon\). Then there exist \(\rho>0\), \(L>0\), and \(\delta_0>0\) such that if \(\delta_\varepsilon<\delta_0\), there is a locally unique section \(h_\varepsilon\in\Gamma^r(E)\) with \(\|h_\varepsilon\|_{C^0}\le\rho\) and \(\operatorname{Lip}(h_\varepsilon)\le L\), whose graph is invariant in the sense that, with \(f_\varepsilon(m):=b_\varepsilon(m,h_\varepsilon(m))\),

\[
h_\varepsilon(f_\varepsilon(m))
=
g_\varepsilon(m,h_\varepsilon(m)).
\]

The map \(f_\varepsilon\) is a \(C^r\) local diffeomorphism \(C^1\)-close to \(f\). If \(\|F_\varepsilon-F_0\|_{C^k}\le C|\varepsilon|\) for \(0\le k\le r\), then \(\|h_\varepsilon\|_{C^k}\le C_k|\varepsilon|\). Uniqueness holds among invariant sections in the same tube satisfying the same Lipschitz and cone admissibility bounds.

A self-contained proof of Theorem 4.1, using only the linear cohomological inverse of Theorem 3.1 and the geometry of Sections 2–3, is given in Appendix D.

---

The graph transform used in Theorem 4.1 is the full two-sided HPS Hadamard graph transform, not the separate stable-forward/unstable-backward component transform. The component equations alone do not imply invariance; the full HPS admissible matching construction is required, and the compatibility of the forward and backward base correspondences is part of the HPS graph-transform theorem. For Banach-space invariant-manifold results using the same graph-transform/Perron architecture, see Bates–Lu [4] and Bates–Lu–Zeng [9]. A naive transform that separately enforces only the stable and unstable component equations is not the HPS transform: affine examples can satisfy those separate equations while failing the full graph equation, because the forward and backward base correspondences need not be inverse to one another. The HPS transform avoids this defect by imposing the simultaneous admissible matching whose fixed points are precisely locally invariant graphs.

A contraction in the Lipschitz norm is not available in general. To see why the Lipschitz norm is incompatible with composition, consider \(M=\mathbb T^1\) and let \(h\) be the triangular wave of period \(p\) with slopes \(\pm 1\), so that \(\operatorname{Lip}(h)=1\). For any shift \(\beta\in\mathbb R\), the difference \(h(\cdot+\beta)-h\) is again piecewise linear with slopes in \(\{-2,0,2\}\), hence

\[
\operatorname{Lip}\bigl(h(\cdot+\beta)-h\bigr)=2
\]

whenever \(\beta\notin\tfrac{p}{2}\mathbb Z\). In particular, the Lipschitz constant of the difference does not tend to \(0\) as \(\beta\to0\): along any sequence \(\beta_n\to0\) avoiding the lattice \(\tfrac{p}{2}\mathbb Z\), one has \(\operatorname{Lip}(h(\cdot+\beta_n)-h)=2\) for all \(n\). Thus composition by a shift arbitrarily close to the identity is not a contraction, nor even a nonexpansive map, on a Lipschitz ball. The same phenomenon occurs for the graph transform: the base preimage map \(b_h^{-1}\) differs from \(f^{-1}\) by a term of size \(O(\|D_\eta b_\varepsilon\|_{C^0}\|h_1-h_2\|_{C^0})\), but when this base displacement is composed with a merely Lipschitz section, the Lipschitz constant of the composition is not controlled by the \(C^0\) distance between the sections. This is why the fixed point is taken in the transported \(C^0\) graph metric and the Lipschitz bound is preserved separately by admissible-graph estimates.

The invariant graph equation has the transported form

\[
\Psi_\varepsilon(h)
:=
P_{f(m)\leftarrow b_{\varepsilon,h}(m)}
\left[
h(b_{\varepsilon,h}(m))
-
g_\varepsilon(m,h(m))
\right]
=
\mathcal Ah+\mathcal N_\varepsilon(h),
\]

where \(\mathcal Ah=h\circ f-Bh\) and

\[
\mathcal N_\varepsilon(h)(m)
=
\Delta_{b_{\varepsilon,h}(m)}^{f(m)}h
-
P_{f(m)\leftarrow b_{\varepsilon,h}(m)}
r_\varepsilon(m,h(m)).
\]

The fixed-point problem is \(h=-\mathcal G\mathcal N_\varepsilon(h)\), with \(\mathcal G\) the cohomological inverse of Theorem 3.1. The graph transform acts as a contraction in the \(C^0\) graph metric, not in the Lipschitz norm, with contraction factor of the schematic form

\[
q
\le
C\left(
e^{-\mathrm{gap}\,N}
+\delta_\varepsilon
+\omega(\rho)
+L
\right),
\]

where the first term comes from the strict normal-hyperbolicity gap and the remaining terms from perturbation size, tube radius, and cone aperture. Higher regularity follows by lifting the graph transform to jet bundles; the \(k\)-jet transform has principal part controlled by \(\|B_m^s\|\,\|Df(m)^{-1}\|^k\) and \(\|(B_m^u)^{-1}\|\,\|Df(m)\|^k\), and the \(k\)-bunching assumption gives contraction on the \(k\)-jet fibres.

---

**Theorem 4.2 (Compact persistence; Fenichel [1], HPS [2], Bates–Lu [4,9]).**

Let \(X\) be a Banach space. Let \(M\subset X\) be a compact finite-dimensional \(C^{r+1}\) embedded submanifold without boundary with a split \(C^{r+1}\) tubular neighbourhood. Let \(F_\varepsilon\) be a family of \(C^{r+1}\) local diffeomorphisms defined on a common neighbourhood of \(M\). Assume \(F_0(M)=M\) and that the invariant splitting \(T_mX=T_mM\oplus E_m^s\oplus E_m^u\) is normally hyperbolic in the sense of Section 2, with the normal cocycle satisfying the \(r\)-bunching inequalities. Then for all sufficiently small \(\varepsilon\) there is a locally unique \(C^r\) embedded manifold \(M_\varepsilon\) near \(M\) with \(F_\varepsilon(M_\varepsilon)=M_\varepsilon\). Moreover there are a \(C^r\) embedding \(K_\varepsilon:M\to X\) with \(M_\varepsilon=K_\varepsilon(M)\) and a \(C^r\) diffeomorphism \(f_\varepsilon:M\to M\) such that \(F_\varepsilon\circ K_\varepsilon=K_\varepsilon\circ f_\varepsilon\). If \(\|F_\varepsilon-F_0\|_{C^r}\le C|\varepsilon|\), then \(\|K_\varepsilon-i_M\|_{C^r}+\|f_\varepsilon-f\|_{C^r}\le C_r|\varepsilon|\). The normal hyperbolicity persists with weakened rates compatible with the original strict gap, and uniqueness holds among admissible graphs in the prescribed tube.

---

**Proof architecture.** In tubular coordinates the hypotheses of Theorem 4.1 are verified directly: the remainder estimates follow from \(C^1\)-closeness and from \(D_\eta b_0(m,0)=0\). Theorem 4.1 then yields the invariant section \(h_\varepsilon\) and the internal map \(f_\varepsilon\), and \(M_\varepsilon:=\Theta(\operatorname{graph}h_\varepsilon)\) satisfies \(F_\varepsilon(M_\varepsilon)\subset M_\varepsilon\). Equality is proved by the compact-manifold argument: \(g_\varepsilon:=f^{-1}\circ f_\varepsilon\) is a \(C^1\) local diffeomorphism close to the identity, hence a covering of degree one, hence a global diffeomorphism, so \(f_\varepsilon(M)=M\) and \(F_\varepsilon(M_\varepsilon)=M_\varepsilon\). The quantitative estimates follow from the parameterized version of Theorem 4.1. Persistence of the splitting follows from roughness of exponential dichotomies: the perturbed normal cocycle \(B_\varepsilon\) is \(C^0\)-close to \(B\), so by the roughness theorem of Palmer [6] it admits an exponential dichotomy with weakened rates, and the perturbed bundles are invariant graphs of linear maps between the original summands. Full details are in [2], [4], [9]; the Banach-bundle roughness statement used here is the fixed-base and close-base form of [6].

---

**Lemma 4.3 (Local diffeomorphism close to the identity).**

Let \(M\) be a compact manifold without boundary. If \(g:M\to M\) is a \(C^1\) local diffeomorphism sufficiently \(C^1\)-close to the identity, then \(g\) is a global \(C^1\) diffeomorphism.

**Proof.** Because \(g\) is a local diffeomorphism, \(g(M)\) is open. Because \(M\) is compact, \(g(M)\) is compact, hence closed. If \(M\) is connected, \(g(M)=M\). If \(M\) is disconnected, the same argument applies componentwise, since \(g\) close to the identity preserves each component. Thus \(g\) is a covering map. Since \(g\) is homotopic to the identity, its covering degree is one componentwise. A degree-one covering is a diffeomorphism. \(\square\)

---

**Remark.** Another common proof strategy constructs local center-stable and center-unstable manifolds and obtains the normally hyperbolic manifold as their transverse intersection,

\[
M_\varepsilon
=
W^{cs}_{\mathrm{loc}}\cap W^{cu}_{\mathrm{loc}}.
\]

That approach is equivalent in spirit but requires separate hypotheses and constructions for the two larger invariant manifolds. It is not used in the proof above.

### 4.1. Supporting classical modules

The persistence architecture uses several classical linear and semi-linear modules. They are available in the literature; the statements are recalled below.

1. **Roughness of exponential dichotomies** (Palmer [6]). If \(B\) has an exponential dichotomy and \(\widetilde B_m=B_m+R_m\) with \(\sup_m\|R_m\|\le\delta\) small, then \(\widetilde B\) has an exponential dichotomy with rate \(\gamma\in(\lambda,1)\), and the perturbed stable and unstable bundles are graphs over the original bundles with graph maps of norm \(O(\delta)\). The fixed-base and close-base versions are in [6]; the close-base version follows by transporting fibres to a common reference bundle.

2. **Parameter expansions** (classical implicit-function theorem). If the graph residual \(\Psi_\varepsilon(h)\) is \(C^{N+1}\) in \((\varepsilon,h)\) with \(D_h\Psi_0(0)=\mathcal A\) invertible, then the invariant section \(h_\varepsilon\) is \(C^{N+1}\) in \(\varepsilon\), and its Taylor coefficients \(\eta_j=\partial_\varepsilon^j h_\varepsilon|_{\varepsilon=0}\) solve recursively
\[
\mathcal A\eta_1=-\partial_\varepsilon\Psi_0(0),
\qquad
\mathcal A\eta_n=-R_n(\eta_1,\dots,\eta_{n-1}),
\quad n\ge2,
\]
where \(R_n\) is a universal finite sum of derivatives of \(\Psi\) applied to lower coefficients. This is the standard consequence of the Banach implicit-function theorem; see [2], [4].

3. **Noncompact bounded-geometry linear theory**. If \(M\) is a complete finite-dimensional \(C^{r+1}\) manifold with bounded geometry and the dichotomy and bunching constants are uniform in the base point, then \(\mathcal A\) is an isomorphism on the bounded section spaces \(C_b^k(M,E)\), with the same Green-operator proof using uniform constants. See [4] and the bounded-geometry framework of [9].

## 5. Strong stable and unstable fibres

Strong fibres require rate-gap assumptions stronger than those for persistence of the manifold alone. The construction is the classical half-line Lyapunov–Perron method; see HPS [2] and, in the Banach-space setting, Bates–Lu [4].

Let \(p\in M_\varepsilon\) and write \(p_n=F_\varepsilon^n(p)\), \(A_n=DF_\varepsilon(p_n)\). Group the tangent directions as \(T_{p_n}X=E^s_{\varepsilon,p_n}\oplus E^{cu}_{\varepsilon,p_n}\), where \(E^{cu}_{\varepsilon,p_n}=T_{p_n}M_\varepsilon\oplus E^u_{\varepsilon,p_n}\). Assume constants \(C>0\), \(\alpha_s>0\), and \(\beta_{cu}\ge0\) with

\[
\|\Phi_\varepsilon^s(n,k)\|\le Ce^{-\alpha_s(n-k)},
\qquad n\ge k,
\]

and

\[
\|\Phi_\varepsilon^{cu}(n,k)\|\le Ce^{\beta_{cu}(k-n)},
\qquad n\le k,
\]

and the strong stable gap condition \(\beta_{cu}<\alpha_s\).

---

**Theorem 5.1 (Strong stable fibres; HPS [2], Bates–Lu [4]).**

Under the above assumptions, for every \(\gamma\in(\beta_{cu},\alpha_s)\) there is a local strong stable fibre \(W^s_{\mathrm{loc}}(p)\) through \(p\), tangent to \(E^s_{\varepsilon,p}\) at \(p\), parametrized by a neighbourhood of the origin in \(E^s_{\varepsilon,p}\), such that points on the fibre track the orbit of \(p\) at rate \(e^{-\gamma n}\). The strong unstable fibres are obtained by applying the same construction to \(F_\varepsilon^{-1}\), with the backward gap condition \(\beta_{cs}<\alpha_u\).

---

**Proof sketch.** Work in coordinates straightened along \(M_\varepsilon\). A nearby orbit is \(p_n+\xi_n\) with \(\xi_n=\xi_n^s+\xi_n^{cu}\), and the nonlinear variational equation is \(\xi_{n+1}=A_n\xi_n+R_n(\xi_n)\) with \(R_n(0)=0\), \(DR_n(0)=0\), and \(\|R_n(\xi)\|\le C\|\xi\|^2\), \(\|DR_n(\xi)\|\le C\|\xi\|\) uniformly for small \(\xi\). In the weighted space \(\ell_\gamma^+=\{\xi:\|\xi\|_\gamma=\sup_{n\ge0}e^{\gamma n}\|\xi_n\|<\infty\}\), the Perron operator

\[
(\mathcal P_a\xi)_n^s
=
\Phi_\varepsilon^s(n,0)a
+
\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}
\Phi_\varepsilon^s(n,k+1)
P^s_{k+1}R_k(\xi_k),
\]

\[
(\mathcal P_a\xi)_n^{cu}
=
-
\sum_{k=n}^{\infty}
\Phi_\varepsilon^{cu}(n,k+1)
P^{cu}_{k+1}R_k(\xi_k)
\]

is a contraction on a small ball \(B_r\subset\ell_\gamma^+\) for \(\|a\|\) small, because the stable convolution is bounded by \(Cr^2\) (using \(\gamma<\alpha_s\)) and the center-unstable convolution by \(Cr^2\sum_{j\ge0}e^{(\beta_{cu}-2\gamma)j}\) (using \(\beta_{cu}<\gamma\)). The fixed point \(\xi(a)\) gives \(W^s_{\mathrm{loc}}(p)=\{p+\xi_0(a)\}\), and tangency follows by differentiating at \(a=0\), where \(D_a\xi_0(0)=\operatorname{id}_{E^s_p}\). Full details are in [2] and [4]. \(\square\)

---

The Perron construction uses only completeness of the weighted sequence space and uniform contraction estimates; it does not require reflexivity of the ambient Banach space. This observation concerns the construction and internal regularity of individual leaves; transverse regularity of the family of leaves and regularity of the phase projection remain separate questions.

Four regularity questions must be distinguished:

1. regularity of each stable leaf in its internal coordinate;
2. regularity of the leaf as a function of the base point \(p\in M_\varepsilon\);
3. regularity of the asymptotic phase projection

\[
\pi^s:W^s_{\mathrm{loc}}(M_\varepsilon)\to M_\varepsilon;
\]

4. regularity of the total lamination.

Internal regularity of the stable leaves in their fibre coordinate \(a\) follows by differentiating the Perron operator with respect to \(a\). For \(C^\ell\) internal regularity, one requires the strong stable bunching condition

\[
\alpha_s>\ell\,\beta_{cu},
\]

or, more invariantly, contractivity of the induced \(\ell\)-jet Perron operator. The nonlinear Faà di Bruno terms are controlled by lower-order derivatives in the induction. The dependence on \(p\) is generally weaker because the reference orbit \(p_n=F_\varepsilon^n(p)\) enters the Perron operator. Under basic normal hyperbolicity the stable foliation is typically continuous, and often Hölder, transversely. Higher transverse regularity and regularity of \(\pi^s\) require separate bunching assumptions; in particular, the full stable foliation is not automatically \(C^r\) transversely.

Iteration extends the local stable fibres globally, provided the orbits remain in the tube in which the construction is valid. Define

\[
W^s(M_\varepsilon)
=
\left\{
y:
F_\varepsilon^n(y)\text{ remains near }M_\varepsilon
\text{ for all }n\ge0
\text{ and approaches }M_\varepsilon
\right\}.
\]

Locally,

\[
W^s(M_\varepsilon)
=
\bigcup_{p\in M_\varepsilon}
W^s_{\mathrm{loc}}(p),
\]

and the invariance property is

\[
F_\varepsilon(W^s(M_\varepsilon))
\subset
W^s(M_\varepsilon).
\]

The stable fibres define an asymptotic phase projection \(\pi^s:W^s_{\mathrm{loc}}(M_\varepsilon)\to M_\varepsilon\): if \(y\in W^s_{\mathrm{loc}}(M_\varepsilon)\), then \(\pi^s(y)\) is the unique point in \(M_\varepsilon\) whose orbit is asymptotic to the orbit of \(y\). For \(n\ge0\),

\[
\operatorname{dist}
\left(
F_\varepsilon^n(y),
F_\varepsilon^n(\pi^s(y))
\right)
\le
C e^{-\gamma n}
\operatorname{dist}(y,M_\varepsilon).
\]

The regularity of \(\pi^s\) is a separate question and requires its own bunching assumptions. The global stable set is the increasing union of pulled-back local laminations and is naturally an immersed lamination, embedded only under additional no-self-intersection or properness assumptions.

The phase map and the transverse regularity of the foliation are governed by the fractional radius of the transverse jet cocycle.

**Theorem 5.2 (Phase-map and transverse regularity).**

Let \(\ell\ge1\) and suppose the strong stable gap condition \(\alpha_s>\ell\,\beta_{cu}\) holds, together with the transverse fractional bunching
\[
\widehat\rho^{\mathrm{tr}}_{\ell,\alpha}<1,
\]
where \(\widehat\rho^{\mathrm{tr}}_{\ell,\alpha}\) is the fractional radius of the \(\ell\)-jet cocycle of the center-unstable bundle \(E^{cu}=T M_\varepsilon\oplus E^u\) over \(f_\varepsilon\). Then the asymptotic phase map \(\pi^s\) is of class \(C^{\ell,\alpha}\), and the strong stable foliation is \(C^{\ell,\alpha}\) transversely.

**Proof.** The phase map is characterised by the equivariance \(\pi^s\circ F_\varepsilon=F_\varepsilon\circ\pi^s\), the normalization \(\pi^s|_{M_\varepsilon}=\mathrm{id}\), and the asymptotic estimate above. Differentiating the equivariance,
\[
D\pi^s(F_\varepsilon(y))\,DF_\varepsilon(y)=DF_\varepsilon(\pi^s(y))\,D\pi^s(y).
\]
In the adapted splitting \(T_yX=E^s_{\varepsilon,y}\oplus E^{cu}_{\varepsilon,y}\), write \(D\pi^s(y)=\Pi_y+R(y)\), where \(\Pi_y\) is the bundle projection onto \(T_{\pi^s(y)}M_\varepsilon\) along \(E^s_{\varepsilon,y}\) and \(R(y)\) is the correction. Substituting and using \(DF_\varepsilon(E^s)\subset E^s\), \(DF_\varepsilon(E^{cu})\subset E^{cu}\), the correction satisfies the cohomological equation
\[
R(F_\varepsilon(y))=\mathcal C_\varepsilon(y)\,R(y)+S(y),
\]
where \(\mathcal C_\varepsilon\) is the \(\ell\)-jet cocycle of the center-unstable bundle \(E^{cu}\) and \(S\) collects the lower-order terms. Since the data is \(C^{r+1,\beta}\) and \(\pi^s\) is \(C^{\ell-1,\alpha}\) by induction, \(S\in C^\alpha\). The fractional radius of \(\mathcal C_\varepsilon\) is \(\widehat\rho^{\mathrm{tr}}_{\ell,\alpha}<1\), so Theorem 6.1 applied to this cohomological equation gives \(R\in C^{\ell-1,\alpha}\), whence \(\pi^s\in C^{\ell,\alpha}\). The base case \(\ell=1\) is the continuity of \(\pi^s\) under the basic gap \(\alpha_s>\beta_{cu}\), which follows from the contraction estimate defining \(\pi^s\). \(\square\)

**Remark.** The transverse regularity of the foliation is generally lower than the internal regularity of the leaves: the leaves are \(C^\ell\) internally under \(\alpha_s>\ell\beta_{cu}\), but the dependence of the leaf on the base point requires the additional transverse fractional bunching \(\widehat\rho^{\mathrm{tr}}_{\ell,\alpha}<1\). The threshold \(\widehat\rho^{\mathrm{tr}}_{\ell,\alpha}=1\) is the transverse regularity spectrum, and its identification with the Mather spectrum of the center-unstable jet cocycle is the content of Problem 4 applied to \(E^{cu}\).

The transverse fractional bunching is also necessary, so the threshold is sharp.

**Theorem 5.3 (Sharpness of the transverse threshold).**

Suppose the transverse jet cocycle \(L^{\mathrm{tr}}_\ell\) has operator spectrum on \(C^\alpha\) that is a union of annuli centred at the origin. Then the phase map \(\pi^s\) is \(C^{\ell,\alpha}\) for all sufficiently small \(C^{r+1,\beta}\) perturbations if and only if
\[
\widehat\rho^{\mathrm{tr}}_{\ell,\alpha}<1.
\]

**Proof.** The sufficiency is Theorem 5.2. For the necessity, suppose \(\widehat\rho^{\mathrm{tr}}_{\ell,\alpha}\ge1\). By Theorem 8.4, the operator \(L^{\mathrm{tr}}_\ell-I\) is not boundedly invertible on \(C^\alpha\), because its annular spectrum meets the unit circle. The \(\ell\)-th transverse derivative of the phase map satisfies the linearized cohomological equation
\[
L^{\mathrm{tr}}_\ell\,D^\ell\pi^s-D^\ell\pi^s=S,
\]
where \(S\) is the lower-order jet term determined by the perturbation. Since \(L^{\mathrm{tr}}_\ell-I\) is not boundedly invertible, there are normalized data \(q_n\in C^\alpha\), \(\|q_n\|_{C^\alpha}=1\), for which the solution operator is unbounded (or the equation is not solvable); choosing perturbations of size \(\delta_n\to0\) whose jet forcing realises \(q_n\), the resulting phase maps satisfy \(\|D^\ell\pi^s\|_{C^\alpha}\to\infty\), so they are not \(C^{\ell,\alpha}\). Letting \(\delta_n\to0\) gives arbitrarily small perturbations that break the \(C^{\ell,\alpha}\) regularity. \(\square\)

**Corollary 5.4.** Under the annular-spectrum hypothesis, the transverse regularity spectrum is exactly \(s^{\mathrm{tr}}_*=\sup\{\ell+\alpha:\widehat\rho^{\mathrm{tr}}_{\ell,\alpha}<1\}\), and the phase map is \(C^{\ell,\alpha}\) for all small perturbations precisely when \(\ell+\alpha<s^{\mathrm{tr}}_*\).

### 5.1. Flows

Let \(\Phi_t^\varepsilon\) be a genuine \(C^{r+1}\) local flow defined for positive and negative time on a neighbourhood of \(M\), with \(\Phi_t^0(M)=M\) for all \(t\). Choose \(T>0\) so that the time-\(T\) map \(F_\varepsilon:=\Phi_T^\varepsilon\) satisfies the hypotheses of Theorem 4.2, and let \(M_\varepsilon\) be the resulting invariant manifold. For \(0\le t\le T\), the manifold \(N_t:=\Phi_t^\varepsilon(M_\varepsilon)\) is invariant under \(F_\varepsilon\) because the flow maps commute; for small \(\varepsilon\) it remains in the same admissible tube with the same Lipschitz and cone bounds, so by local uniqueness \(N_t=M_\varepsilon\). Hence \(\Phi_t^\varepsilon(M_\varepsilon)=M_\varepsilon\) for \(0\le t\le T\), and by the flow property for all \(t\in\mathbb R\). The time-map reduction requires full-flow invariance of the unperturbed manifold: if only \(\Phi_T^0(M)=M\) is known, then \(\Phi_t^0(M)\) for \(0<t<T\) may leave the tubular neighbourhood, and the uniqueness argument cannot be applied.

In continuous time, the strong stable Perron operator for the deviation equation

\[
\dot\xi(t)=A(t)\xi(t)+R(t,\xi(t))
\]

is

\[
\xi^s(t)
=
\Phi^s(t,0)a
+
\int_0^t
\Phi^s(t,s)P^s(s)R(s,\xi(s))\,ds,
\]

and

\[
\xi^{cu}(t)
=
-
\int_t^\infty
\Phi^{cu}(t,s)P^{cu}(s)R(s,\xi(s))\,ds.
\]

The same rate-gap condition is required: the stable decay rate must dominate the backward growth rate of the center-unstable complement.

## 6. The fractional jet spectrum

We prove a fractional jet-spectrum theorem for the cohomological operator \(\mathcal A\). The result is independent of the nonlinear HPS matching mechanism; it concerns only the Green inverse for \(\mathcal A\). The integer bunching assumptions of Section 2.2 are sufficient criteria; the intrinsic condition is the spectral radius of the exact Green summand operators acting on Hölder jet sections.

### 6.1. Hölder Banach-bundle setting

Let \(M\) be a compact finite-dimensional \(C^{r+1,\beta}\) manifold without boundary, where \(0<\beta\le1\). Let \(E\to M\) be a \(C^{r+1,\beta}\) Banach bundle. Fix either a \(C^{r,\beta}\) connection or a finite \(C^{r+1,\beta}\) bundle atlas with uniformly controlled transition maps. Let \(f\in\operatorname{Diff}^{r+1,\beta}(M)\), and let \(B_m:E_m\to E_{f(m)}\) be a \(C^{r,\beta}\) bundle automorphism. Assume a \(C^{r,\beta}\) invariant splitting \(E=E^s\oplus E^u\) with uniformly bounded \(C^{r,\beta}\) projections, and a uniform exponential dichotomy over \(f\). Let

\[
s=k+\alpha,
\qquad
0<\alpha<1,
\]

where \(k\) is a nonnegative integer and \(k+\alpha\le r+\beta\). The space \(C^{k,\alpha}(M,E)\) is defined using the fixed connection or finite atlas. For \(k=0\), this is the usual Hölder section space \(C^\alpha(M,E)\).

### 6.2. Green summand operators and fractional radii

Let

\[
\mathcal J_k(E):=
\operatorname{Sym}^k(T^*M;E)
\]

be the bundle of symmetric \(k\)-linear covariant tensors with values in \(E\). For \(k=0\), set \(\mathcal J_0(E)=E\). The principal \(k\)-jet cocycle over \(f\) is

\[
\mathscr B_k(m)J
=
B_m\circ J\circ(Df(m)^{-1})^{\otimes k},
\]

mapping \(\mathcal J_k(E)_m\) to \(\mathcal J_k(E)_{f(m)}\).

The stable Green summand of length \(n\) is the operator

\[
\mathcal S_{k,n}^+:
C^\alpha(M,
\mathcal J_k(E)^s)
\to
C^\alpha(M,
\mathcal J_k(E)^s)
\]

which assigns to a jet-forcing section its stable contribution transported from time \(-n\) to time \(0\). In local notation,

\[
(\mathcal S_{k,n}^+Q)(m)
=
\Phi^s(0,-n;m)
\,Q(f^{-n}m)
\circ
(Df^{-n}(m))^{\otimes k},
\]

with the appropriate bundle transports and projections included. Similarly, the unstable Green summand of length \(n\) is

\[
\mathcal S_{k,n}^-:
C^\alpha(M,
\mathcal J_k(E)^u)
\to
C^\alpha(M,
\mathcal J_k(E)^u),
\]

with local form

\[
(\mathcal S_{k,n}^-Q)(m)
=
\Phi^u(0,n;m)
\,Q(f^n m)
\circ
(Df^{n}(m))^{\otimes k}.
\]

These definitions are invariantly interpreted using the fixed connection or atlas. Their operator norms on \(C^\alpha\) include all Hölder variation of cocycle products, projections, derivative iterates, and transports. Under the \(C^{r,\beta}\) regularity hypotheses of Section 6.1, each \(\mathcal S_{k,n}^\pm\) is a bounded operator on \(C^\alpha\); the Hölder seminorm estimates are recorded in Appendix A.

Define the fractional jet radii

\[
\widehat\rho_{k,\alpha}^+
=
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\|\mathcal S_{k,n}^+\|_{C^\alpha\to C^\alpha}^{1/n},
\]

and

\[
\widehat\rho_{k,\alpha}^-
=
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\|\mathcal S_{k,n}^-\|_{C^\alpha\to C^\alpha}^{1/n}.
\]

Set

\[
\widehat\rho_{k,\alpha}
=
\max\{
\widehat\rho_{k,\alpha}^+,
\widehat\rho_{k,\alpha}^-
\}.
\]

### 6.3. The fractional cohomological inverse theorem

**Theorem 6.1 (Hölder jet-spectrum inverse).**

Assume the \(C^0\) exponential dichotomy. Let \(k+\alpha\le r+\beta\), with \(0<\alpha<1\). If

\[
\widehat\rho_{k,\alpha}<1,
\]

then

\[
\mathcal A:
C^{k,\alpha}(M,E)
\to
C^{k,\alpha}(M,f^*E),
\qquad
(\mathcal A\zeta)(m)=
\zeta(f(m))-B_m\zeta(m),
\]

is an isomorphism. Moreover,

\[
\|\mathcal A^{-1}q\|_{C^{k,\alpha}}
\le
C_{k,\alpha}
\|q\|_{C^{k,\alpha}}.
\]

The same statement holds on noncompact bounded-geometry bases in \(C_b^{k,\alpha}\) spaces if all geometric and operator-norm estimates are uniform.

### 6.4. Proof of Theorem 6.1

#### 6.4.1. Zeroth-order inverse

The \(C^0\) inverse is the ordinary Green operator. Along an orbit \(m_n=f^n(m)\), write

\[
q_n=q(m_n)\in E_{m_{n+1}}.
\]

Decompose in the target fibre:

\[
q_n^\sigma=P_{m_{n+1}}^\sigma q_n.
\]

The unique bounded solution of

\[
\zeta_{n+1}-B_{m_n}\zeta_n=q_n
\]

is

\[
\zeta_n^s
=
\sum_{j=-\infty}^{n-1}
\Phi^s(n,j+1)q_j^s,
\]

and

\[
\zeta_n^u
=
-
\sum_{j=n}^{\infty}
\Phi^u(n,j+1)q_j^u.
\]

This gives a bounded inverse on \(C^0\), and uniqueness follows from the usual bounded homogeneous-solution argument.

#### 6.4.2. Hölder Green lemma

**Lemma 6.2 (Hölder Green lemma).** Let \(F\to M\) be a \(C^\alpha\) Banach bundle and let \(C_m:F_m\to F_{f(m)}\) be a cocycle with a \(C^0\) exponential dichotomy. Suppose the exact stable and unstable Green summand operators on \(C^\alpha\) sections satisfy

\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\|\mathcal S_n^+\|_{C^\alpha\to C^\alpha}^{1/n}<1,
\]

and

\[
\limsup_{n\to\infty}
\|\mathcal S_n^-\|_{C^\alpha\to C^\alpha}^{1/n}<1.
\]

Then the Green operator is bounded on \(C^\alpha\).

**Proof.** Choose \(\theta<1\) larger than both limsup values. Then, for all sufficiently large \(n\),

\[
\|\mathcal S_n^\pm\|_{C^\alpha\to C^\alpha}
\le
C\theta^n.
\]

The finitely many small \(n\) terms are harmless, and the Green series is a normally convergent series in the Banach space \(C^\alpha(M,F)\). The limit is the Green operator, and the normal convergence gives the bound \(\|\mathcal G\|_{C^\alpha\to C^\alpha}\le C(1-\theta)^{-1}\). \(\square\)

#### 6.4.3. Application to the \(k\)-jet equation

For the cohomological equation

\[
\zeta\circ f-B\zeta=q,
\]

the \(k\)-th covariant derivative has principal part

\[
D^k\zeta(f(m))
\circ Df(m)^{\otimes k}
-
B_mD^k\zeta(m).
\]

Equivalently, with \(J=D^k\zeta\),

\[
J(f(m))-\mathscr B_k(m)J(m)=Q_k(m),
\]

where \(Q_k\) contains \(D^kq\) and lower-order terms involving derivatives of \(f\), \(B\), the connection, and \(\zeta\) up to order \(k-1\).

The integer \(C^k\) cohomological inverse theorem (Theorem 3.1) gives

\[
\|\zeta\|_{C^k}
\le
C_k\|q\|_{C^k}.
\]

Because \(q\in C^{k,\alpha}\), and because the data have regularity at least \(C^{k,\alpha}\), the lower-order terms satisfy

\[
\|Q_k\|_{C^\alpha}
\le
C_{k,\alpha}\|q\|_{C^{k,\alpha}}.
\]

By definition, the stable and unstable Green summands for the \(k\)-jet equation have radii bounded by \(\widehat\rho_{k,\alpha}<1\). The Hölder Green lemma (Lemma 6.2) therefore gives

\[
\|D^k\zeta\|_{C^\alpha}
\le
C_{k,\alpha}\|Q_k\|_{C^\alpha}
\le
C_{k,\alpha}
\|q\|_{C^{k,\alpha}}.
\]

Together with the integer estimate, this yields

\[
\|\zeta\|_{C^{k,\alpha}}
\le
C_{k,\alpha}\|q\|_{C^{k,\alpha}}.
\]

#### 6.4.4. Convergence without a priori regularity

Let \(G_Nq\) denote the Green series truncated at orbit lengths at most \(N\). Since the summands are \(C^{k,\alpha}\), each \(G_Nq\) is \(C^{k,\alpha}\). The argument of Section 6.4.3 applies uniformly to the finite sums and shows that \(G_Nq\) is Cauchy in \(C^{k,\alpha}\). The uniform limit is the \(C^0\) Green solution \(Gq\). Hence \(Gq\in C^{k,\alpha}\), and the estimate follows. This avoids assuming differentiability of the \(C^0\) solution in advance; the detailed truncation estimates are recorded in Appendix B.

Uniqueness in \(C^{k,\alpha}\) follows from uniqueness in \(C^0\). Thus \(\mathcal A\) is an isomorphism on \(C^{k,\alpha}\). \(\square\)

### 6.5. Relation with one-step Hölder bunching

A stronger but more concrete sufficient condition is obtained by estimating the exact operator norms \(\|\mathcal S_{k,n}^\pm\|_{C^\alpha\to C^\alpha}\). If the cocycle products, projections, derivative iterates, and bundle transports have bounded distortion so that

\[
\|\mathcal S_{k,n}^+\|_{C^\alpha\to C^\alpha}
\le
C\theta^n
\]

and

\[
\|\mathcal S_{k,n}^-\|_{C^\alpha\to C^\alpha}
\le
C\theta^n
\]

for some \(\theta<1\), then \(\widehat\rho_{k,\alpha}<1\). In particular, the usual pointwise real-order bunching inequalities imply the theorem when supplemented by the corresponding bounded-distortion estimates for the iterated jet cocycles. The bounded-distortion verification under the \(C^{r,\beta}\) hypotheses is given in Appendix A; the jet-calculus identities underlying the principal part \(\mathscr B_k\) are verified in Appendix C.

Thus the operator radius is the intrinsic formulation, while one-step norm-bunching is a convenient sufficient condition in adapted settings.

**Intuition.** The integer bunching inequalities \(\|\Phi^s(n,0)\|\,\|Df^{-n}\|^k\le C\theta^n\) are sufficient but not sharp: they treat the normal contraction, the tangent expansion, and the bundle geometry as separate scalar rates, and they ignore the Hölder distortion of the cocycle products, the projections, and the transports. The fractional radius \(\widehat\rho_{k,\alpha}\) measures the exact exponential growth rate of the Green summand operator on the Hölder jet bundle, with all of these factors combined. When the base dynamics is expanding, the tangent factor \(\|Df^{-n}\|^k\) grows exponentially, and the fractional radius can exceed \(1\) even when the normal contraction is strong; this is the mechanism by which the regularity threshold \(s_*\) becomes finite. When the base is isometric, the tangent distortion is absent, and the fractional radius reduces to the normal contraction rate, so no regularity is lost. The fractional spectrum thus interpolates between the "no distortion" case (isometric base) and the "full distortion" case (expanding base), and the threshold \(s_*\) marks the transition.

## 7. Periodic jet-resonance obstruction

Theorem 6.1 gives a sufficient condition for \(C^{k,\alpha}\) invertibility. We prove a converse obstruction: at a periodic orbit, the linearized cohomological equation can fail to be surjective on \(C^k\) even when the \(C^0\) equation is invertible.

### 7.1. The resonance obstruction

Let \(p\) be a periodic point of prime period \(N\). Let

\[
\mathfrak J_{\le k,p}
=
\bigoplus_{j=0}^{k}
\operatorname{Sym}^j(T_p^*M;E_p)
\]

be the full jet space up to order \(k\). The full triangular jet return map of the cohomological equation is the operator

\[
\mathfrak R_{\le k}(p):
\mathfrak J_{\le k,p}
\to
\mathfrak J_{\le k,p}.
\]

Its diagonal order-\(j\) block is the principal jet return map

\[
\mathscr R_j(p)J
=
\Phi(N,0;p)
\circ J\circ
(Df^N(p)^{-1})^{\otimes j}.
\]

Lower-order jet terms appear in the upper triangular blocks.

**Proposition 7.1 (Periodic jet-resonance obstruction).**

Assume the zeroth-order normal cocycle has an exponential dichotomy, so that \(\mathcal A\) is invertible on \(C^0\). If, for some periodic point \(p\),

\[
I-
\mathfrak R_{\le k}(p)
\]

is not surjective, then

\[
\mathcal A:C^k(M,E)\to C^k(M,f^*E)
\]

is not surjective.

If all lower-order blocks are nonresonant and

\[
I-
\mathscr R_k(p)
\]

is not surjective, the same conclusion follows.

**Proof.** Suppose \(\mathcal A:C^k(M,E)\to C^k(M,f^*E)\) were onto. Choose a finite jet datum for \(q\) along the periodic orbit such that the induced right-hand side in the periodic jet equation lies outside the range of

\[
I-
\mathfrak R_{\le k}(p).
\]

This is possible because the latter operator is not surjective. Since the periodic orbit is finite and \(M\) is finite-dimensional, a \(C^k\) section realizing these finite jets exists by a local bump-function construction in disjoint coordinate charts.

Let \(\zeta\in C^k(M,E)\) solve \(\mathcal A\zeta=q\). Taking jets of order \(\le k\) along the periodic orbit gives a linear periodic system. Iterating around the orbit yields

\[
(I-
\mathfrak R_{\le k}(p))Z=Q,
\]

where \(Z\) is the jet of \(\zeta\) at \(p\), and \(Q\) is the prescribed jet datum of \(q\). This contradicts the choice of \(Q\). Hence \(\mathcal A\) is not onto on \(C^k\). \(\square\)

**Intuition.** Around a periodic orbit of period \(N\), the cohomological equation becomes a linear system \((I-\mathfrak R_k(p))Z=Q\) on the jet space, where \(Z\) is the jet of \(\zeta\) at \(p\) and \(Q\) the prescribed jet of \(q\). If the return map \(\mathfrak R_k(p)\) has eigenvalue \(1\), the system is not solvable for all \(Q\), and \(\mathcal A\) is not onto. The eigenvalue-\(1\) condition is a resonance between the normal contraction and the tangent expansion: the normal multiplier and the tangent derivative conspire so that the product of the normal multiplier with \((Df^N)^{-k}\) equals \(1\). The two-cycle witness of Section 7.3 realizes this with normal multiplier \(1/2\), tangent derivative \(1/2\), and \(k=1\), where the \(C^0\) equation is invertible (determinant \(-3/4\)) but the \(C^1\) equation is not (determinant \(0\)). The resonance is thus a "jet-level" phenomenon: it can appear at order \(k\) even when the base dynamics is hyperbolic and the \(C^0\) cohomological equation is invertible.

### 7.2. Compact returns and the Fredholm alternative

The obstruction of Proposition 7.1 is stated as failure of surjectivity of

\[
I-\mathfrak R_{\le k}(p).
\]

In general Banach spaces this is the correct formulation: non-surjectivity need not be caused by an eigenvector. In compact or smoothing settings, however, the obstruction becomes an ordinary eigenvalue resonance.

**Theorem 7.2 (Compact jet-return Fredholm alternative).**

Let \(Y\) be a Banach space and let \(K:Y\to Y\) be compact. If \(I-K\) is not surjective, then \(1\) is an eigenvalue of \(K\), with finite algebraic multiplicity.

**Proof.** This is the Riesz–Schauder theorem: if \(I-K\) is not surjective, then \(I-K\) is not invertible, so \(1\in\sigma(K)\). Since \(1\ne0\) and \(K\) is compact, \(1\) is an eigenvalue of finite algebraic multiplicity. \(\square\)

**Corollary 7.3.**

If the periodic jet-return operator

\[
\mathfrak R_{\le k}(p):\mathfrak J_{\le k,p}\to\mathfrak J_{\le k,p}
\]

is compact and if

\[
I-\mathfrak R_{\le k}(p)
\]

is not surjective, then there exists a nonzero jet

\[
J\in\mathfrak J_{\le k,p}
\]

such that

\[
\mathfrak R_{\le k}(p)J=J.
\]

Thus, in compact-return settings, the periodic obstruction is an eigenvalue-one resonance of finite algebraic multiplicity.

**Corollary 7.4 (Parabolic compact-return case).**

Assume a parabolic or smoothing setting in which the linear time-return map along a periodic orbit is compact on the chosen normal fibre space, for example because the sectorial generator has compact resolvent and the return time is positive. Since the tangent jet factors are finite-dimensional, the induced finite jet-return map \(\mathfrak R_{\le k}(p)\) is compact on the Banach jet fibre. Therefore any failure of surjectivity of \(I-\mathfrak R_{\le k}(p)\) is equivalent to the existence of an eigen-jet with eigenvalue \(1\).

Without compactness, Proposition 7.1 should not be strengthened in this way: the obstruction may lie in point, continuous, or residual spectrum. The correct general statement remains non-surjectivity of \(I-\mathfrak R_{\le k}(p)\).

### 7.3. A witness: first-jet resonance on a two-cycle

Let the base have a two-cycle and consider scalar fibres. Take normal multipliers

\[
B_0=B_1=\frac12
\]

and tangent derivatives

\[
Df_0=Df_1=\frac12.
\]

The \(C^0\) cohomological matrix is

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
-1/2&1\\
1&-1/2
\end{pmatrix},
\]

whose determinant is

\[
-\frac34\ne0.
\]

Thus the \(C^0\) equation is invertible. But the first-jet matrix is

\[
\begin{pmatrix}
-1&1\\
1&-1
\end{pmatrix},
\]

whose determinant is zero. The first-jet return map has eigenvalue one, so the \(C^1\) cohomological equation is not onto. This is the simplest periodic jet-resonance obstruction.

## 8. The regularity spectrum

### 8.1. Definition of the spectrum

Define

\[
s_*=\sup\{k+\alpha:
\widehat\rho_{k,\alpha}<1\}.
\tag{8.1}
\]

Then the Green inverse is bounded on every noninteger Hölder space \(C^{k,\alpha}\) with

\[
k+\alpha<s_*
\]

for which the data have the required regularity. Periodic jet resonances obstruct surjectivity at resonant integer orders. Thus the fractional jet spectrum describes the linear regularity threshold for the cohomological inverse.

### 8.2. Model examples

**Example 8.1 (Hyperbolic toral base with scalar attracting normal direction).**

Let

\[
A=
\begin{pmatrix}
2&1\\
1&1
\end{pmatrix}
\]

act on \(\mathbb T^2\). Its expanding eigenvalue is

\[
\Lambda=\frac{3+\sqrt5}{2}>1.
\]

Let \(E=\mathbb T^2\times\mathbb R\), and let the normal cocycle be scalar multiplication by

\[
0<\lambda<1.
\]

Then the cohomological equation is

\[
\zeta(Ax)-\lambda\zeta(x)=q(x).
\]

The stable Green summands at real order \(s=k+\alpha\) have growth rate

\[
\widehat\rho_s=
\lambda\Lambda^s.
\]

Consequently the Green inverse is bounded on every \(C^s\) space with

\[
s<\frac{\log(1/\lambda)}{
\log\Lambda}.
\]

Thus the regularity threshold is

\[
s_*=
\frac{\log(1/\lambda)}{
\log\Lambda}.
\]

If \(\lambda=\Lambda^{-N}\) for an integer \(N\), then the principal order-\(N\) jet return map at the fixed point \(0\in\mathbb T^2\) has eigenvalue one. Hence the periodic jet-resonance obstruction applies at order \(N\). This gives an explicit family where the fractional spectrum predicts the loss of integer-order surjectivity at the threshold.

**Example 8.2 (Isometric base dynamics).**

If \(f\) is an isometry and the bundle transports, projections, and cocycle products have uniformly bounded Hölder constants, then

\[
\operatorname{Lip}(f^{\pm n})=1.
\]

For a scalar normally attracting cocycle \(B_m=\lambda\), \(0<\lambda<1\), the fractional radius is simply

\[
\widehat\rho_s=\lambda
\]

for all \(s\) allowed by the regularity of the data. Thus the cohomological inverse loses no regularity beyond the regularity already present in the coefficients. This contrasts with the hyperbolic toral example, where tangent distortion creates a finite regularity threshold.

### 8.3. Nonlinear Hölder persistence

**Theorem 8.3 (Nonlinear Hölder regularity at fractional thresholds).**

Let the data be \(C^{r+1,\beta}\), and let \(s_*\) be the fractional linear regularity spectrum of equation (8.1). Suppose
\[
k+\alpha<s_*
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
k+\alpha\le r+\beta.
\]
Then the invariant section \(h_\varepsilon\) of Theorem 4.1 belongs to \(C^{k,\alpha}\), and if \(\|F_\varepsilon-F_0\|_{C^{r+1,\beta}}\le C|\varepsilon|\) then
\[
\|h_\varepsilon\|_{C^{k,\alpha}}\le C_{k,\alpha}|\varepsilon|.
\]

**Proof.** Existence and the \(C^r\) bound follow from Theorem 4.1, whose self-contained proof is in Appendix D. It remains to promote the regularity to the fractional class \(C^{k,\alpha}\). We argue by induction on \(k\).

For \(k=0\), the section \(h_\varepsilon\) is Lipschitz by Theorem 4.1, and the invariance equation, written in the transported form (D.1), is a cohomological equation \(\mathcal A_\varepsilon h_\varepsilon=\mathcal N_\varepsilon\) with \(\mathcal A_\varepsilon\) the cohomological operator of the perturbed base map \(f_\varepsilon\). The perturbed normal cocycle \(B_\varepsilon\) is a small perturbation of \(B\), so its fractional radius satisfies \(\widehat\rho_{0,\alpha}^\varepsilon<1\) for small \(\varepsilon\) whenever \(\widehat\rho_{0,\alpha}<1\), which holds since \(s_*>0\). Theorem 6.1 applied to \(\mathcal A_\varepsilon\) gives \(h_\varepsilon\in C^\alpha\), with \(\|h_\varepsilon\|_{C^\alpha}\le C\|\mathcal N_\varepsilon\|_{C^\alpha}\le C|\varepsilon|\).

Suppose \(h_\varepsilon\in C^{k-1,\alpha}\) for some \(1\le k\le r\), and assume \(k+\alpha<s_*\). Differentiating the invariance equation
\[
h_\varepsilon(f_\varepsilon(m))=g_\varepsilon(m,h_\varepsilon(m))
\]
\(k\) times covariantly, the \(k\)-th derivative \(J=D^k h_\varepsilon\) satisfies
\[
J(f_\varepsilon(m))\circ Df_\varepsilon(m)^{\otimes k}
-
\mathscr B_k^\varepsilon(m)J(m)
=
Q_k(m),
\tag{8.3}
\]
where \(\mathscr B_k^\varepsilon\) is the principal \(k\)-jet cocycle of the perturbed system and \(Q_k\) collects \(D^k g_\varepsilon\) together with lower-order terms involving derivatives of \(f_\varepsilon\), \(B_\varepsilon\), the connection, and \(h_\varepsilon\) up to order \(k-1\). Because \(h_\varepsilon\in C^{k-1,\alpha}\) and the data is \(C^{r+1,\beta}\) with \(k+\alpha\le r+\beta\), the Faà di Bruno expansion of Appendix C gives \(Q_k\in C^\alpha\), with
\[
\|Q_k\|_{C^\alpha}\le C\bigl(\|F_\varepsilon\|_{C^{r+1,\beta}}+\|h_\varepsilon\|_{C^{k-1,\alpha}}\bigr).
\]
The perturbed jet cocycle \(\mathscr B_k^\varepsilon\) differs from \(\mathscr B_k\) by \(O(\delta_\varepsilon)\) in the \(C^\alpha\) operator norm, so its fractional radius satisfies \(\widehat\rho_{k,\alpha}^\varepsilon<1\) for small \(\varepsilon\) whenever \(\widehat\rho_{k,\alpha}<1\), which holds since \(k+\alpha<s_*\). Theorem 6.1 applied to the \(k\)-jet equation (8.3) gives
\[
\|D^k h_\varepsilon\|_{C^\alpha}
\le
C_{k,\alpha}\|Q_k\|_{C^\alpha}
\le
C_{k,\alpha},
\]
so \(h_\varepsilon\in C^{k,\alpha}\). This completes the induction and the proof. \(\square\)

**Sharpness.** By Theorem 8.4, for cocycles whose operator spectrum on \(C^\alpha\) is a union of annuli centred at the origin, the condition \(k+\alpha<s_*\) is also necessary for bounded invertibility of the linearization. In that case the threshold \(s_*\) is sharp: the invariant section cannot be expected to be \(C^{k,\alpha}\) for \(k+\alpha>s_*\) by any argument that factors through the bounded inverse of the linearized cohomological operator.

**Resonance obstruction.** If an integer order \(k\) is obstructed by a periodic jet resonance in the sense of Section 7, then the linearized cohomological operator is not onto on \(C^k\). In that case one cannot expect a uniform \(C^k\) implicit-function-theorem proof of the invariance equation, although special nonlinear perturbations may still admit special solutions.

### 8.4. Spectral factorization and the converse

The fractional radius \(\widehat\rho_{k,\alpha}\) is the spectral radius of a weighted shift. This yields an operator-theoretic resolution of the converse problem and identifies the radii with the Mather spectrum of the jet cocycle.

**Factorization.** Let \(M:\Gamma(E)\to\Gamma(f^*E)\) be the fibrewise multiplication \((M\zeta)(m)=B_m\zeta(m)\), and let \(T:\Gamma(E)\to\Gamma(f^*E)\) be the composition \((T\zeta)(m)=\zeta(f(m))\). Both are invertible with bounded inverses. The cohomological operator factors as

\[
\mathcal A=T-M=M(L-I),
\qquad
L:=M^{-1}T,
\qquad
(L\zeta)(m)=B_m^{-1}\zeta(f(m)).
\]

Since \(M\) is invertible with bounded inverse, \(\mathcal A\) is boundedly invertible on \(C^{k,\alpha}\) if and only if \(L-I\) is, i.e. if and only if \(1\notin\sigma(L)\) with \(\operatorname{dist}(1,\sigma(L))>0\). The operator \(L\) preserves the splitting, \(L=L^s\oplus L^u\), and its principal part on \(k\)-jets is

\[
(L_kJ)(m)=B_m^{-1}\,J(f(m))\circ Df(m)^{\otimes k}.
\]

**Identification of the radii.** The \(n\)-th stable Green summand is the stable part of the backward jet iterate. A direct computation gives \((L_k^{-n}J)(m)=\Phi(0,-n)\,J(f^{-n}m)\circ(Df^{-n}(m))^{\otimes k}\), whose stable projection is the summand of Section 6.2. Hence

\[
\mathcal S_{k,n}^+=P^s(L_k^{-1})^n|_{E^s},
\qquad
\mathcal S_{k,n}^-=P^u(L_k)^n|_{E^u},
\]

and, taking spectral radii,

\[
\widehat\rho_{k,\alpha}^+
=
r\big(L_k^{-1}\big|_{C^{k,\alpha}(\mathcal J_k^s)}\big),
\qquad
\widehat\rho_{k,\alpha}^-
=
r\big(L_k\big|_{C^{k,\alpha}(\mathcal J_k^u)}\big).
\tag{8.2}
\]

---

**Theorem 8.4 (Spectral resolution of the cohomological inverse).**

(i) \(\widehat\rho_{k,\alpha}<1\) if and only if

\[
\sigma(L_k|_{E^s})\subset\{|z|>1\}
\qquad\text{and}\qquad
\sigma(L_k|_{E^u})\subset\{|z|<1\},
\]

a strict spectral gap of \(L_k\) at the unit circle.

(ii) If (i) holds, then \(\mathcal A\) is boundedly invertible on \(C^{k,\alpha}\).

(iii) \(\mathcal A\) is boundedly invertible on \(C^{k,\alpha}\) if and only if \(1\notin\sigma(L_k)\) with \(\operatorname{dist}(1,\sigma(L_k))>0\).

(iv) Condition (i) is sufficient but not necessary for (iii) in general. The obstruction is a spectral gap at the point \(z=1\) in an operator spectrum that meets the unit circle away from \(1\). When the operator spectrum of \(L_k\) on \(C^\alpha\) is a union of annuli centred at the origin — as for isometric base dynamics and for hyperbolic toral base dynamics — it cannot meet the unit circle without containing the whole circle, in particular the point \(1\); in that case (i) and (iii) are equivalent, so the converse holds.

**Proof.** (i) From (8.2) and \(r(L_k^{-1}|_{E^s})=1/\min\{|z|:z\in\sigma(L_k|_{E^s})\}\), the inequality \(\widehat\rho_{k,\alpha}^+<1\) is equivalent to \(\min\{|z|:z\in\sigma(L_k|_{E^s})\}>1\), i.e. \(\sigma(L_k|_{E^s})\subset\{|z|>1\}\); similarly for the unstable part. (ii) Under (i), the Neumann series for \((L_k-I)^{-1}\) converges normally on \(C^{k,\alpha}\), and the lower-order jet terms are controlled by the integer \(C^{k-1}\) estimate of Theorem 3.1. (iii) This is the factorization \(\mathcal A=M(L-I)\) together with the boundedness of \(M\) and \(M^{-1}\), applied to the jet equation that governs \(C^{k,\alpha}\) solvability. (iv) A union of annuli centred at \(0\) meets \(\{|z|=1\}\) only if it contains the entire unit circle; hence, if it meets the unit circle but does not contain \(1\), the spectrum must be non-annular. \(\square\)

---

**Mather spectrum and the radii.** Equation (8.2) identifies the fractional radii with the outer radii of the Sacker–Sell (Mather) spectrum of the jet cocycle \(L_k\) acting on \(C^\alpha(M,\mathcal J_k(E))\): the stable radius \(\widehat\rho_{k,\alpha}^+\) is the reciprocal of the inner radius of the stable part \(\sigma(L_k|_{E^s})\), and the unstable radius \(\widehat\rho_{k,\alpha}^-\) is the outer radius of the unstable part \(\sigma(L_k|_{E^u})\). This exhibits the fractional jet spectrum as a spectral invariant of the cocycle, complementing the sufficient-condition Theorem 6.1 and answering the spectral-interpretation problem.

**Periodic orbits.** For a periodic orbit of prime period \(N\), the spectrum \(\sigma(L_k)\) is the set of eigenvalues of the return map \(\mathfrak R_k(p)\), and \(1\in\sigma(L_k)\) if and only if \(\mathfrak R_k(p)\) has eigenvalue \(1\). This recovers the resonance obstruction of Proposition 7.1 from the factorization, and shows that the periodic obstruction is precisely the failure of the spectral gap at \(z=1\).

**Spectral picture.** The situation of Theorem 8.4 is visualised in the complex plane:

```
        Im
         |        stable annulus (outside unit circle)
         |      .-''''''-.
         |    .'  shaded  '.
         |   /   region     \
         |  |   r1<=|z|<=r2  |
    -----+--|----------------|------ Re
         |  |    (unit circle  |
         |   \    dashed)     /
         |    '.           .'
         |      '-.....-'
         |         ^
         |         | z=1 (resonance point)
```

The unit circle \(|z|=1\) is dashed; the annular Mather spectrum of \(L_k\) is the shaded region; the point \(z=1\) is the resonance. Theorem 8.4 says that \(\mathcal A\) is boundedly invertible iff \(1\) lies outside the spectrum. The strict gap \(\widehat\rho_{k,\alpha}<1\) means the stable annulus lies entirely outside the unit circle and the unstable annulus entirely inside, so \(1\) lies in the gap. If the annulus crosses the unit circle (as in the hyperbolic toral example at \(s=s_*\)), then \(1\) lies in the spectrum, and the cohomological operator is not invertible.

## 9. Extensions

The compact invertible theorem serves as the model case. The same architecture extends to several broader classes of systems. In each case one replaces the discrete Green operator by the appropriate linear inverse and adapts the function spaces to the dynamics. Each extension requires its own theorem-level hypotheses. Without additional hypotheses, the compact invertible theorem does not cover noninvertible parabolic semiflows, unbounded linear generators, noncompact invariant manifolds, Banach-dimensional invariant manifolds, delay equations, random or nonautonomous cocycles, quasilinear equations with derivative loss, manifolds with boundary, or transverse \(C^r\) regularity of stable or unstable foliations without additional bunching.

| Setting | Linear inverse | Function space | Geometric hypothesis | Analytic hypothesis |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Compact invertible map | discrete Green operator | \(C^k(M,E)\) | compact \(M\), split tubular neighbourhood | bunching |
| Noncompact finite-dimensional manifold | discrete Green operator with uniform constants | \(C_b^k(M,E)\) | bounded geometry | uniform bunching |
| Banach-dimensional manifold | discrete Green operator on Banach bundles | \(C_b^k(M,E)\) | split tubular neighbourhood, complemented normal bundle, adapted metric | bunching in adapted metric |
| Parabolic semiflow | analytic semigroup Perron integral | weighted mild-solution spaces | interpolation scale, sectorial generator | sectorial dichotomy, smoothing |
| Random cocycle | random Green operator | tempered weighted spaces | measurable bundle, tempered metric | random or tempered dichotomy |
| Quasilinear PDE | tame or maximal-regularity inverse | scales \(X_s\) | scale of Banach spaces, smoothing operators | derivative recovery or Nash–Moser |

**Finite-dimensional singular perturbations.** For systems \(\dot x=f(x,y,\varepsilon)\), \(\dot y=\varepsilon g(x,y,\varepsilon)\) with critical manifold \(M_0=\{f(x,y,0)=0\}\), normal hyperbolicity means the fast linearization \(D_xf(x,y,0)\) has spectrum bounded away from the imaginary axis. If \(M_\varepsilon\) is written as a graph \(x=h_\varepsilon(y)\), the reduced equation is exactly \(\dot y=\varepsilon g(h_\varepsilon(y),y,\varepsilon)\). See Fenichel [1].

**Parabolic semiflows.** Let \(A\) be a sectorial operator on a Banach space \(X_0\) generating an analytic semigroup, with interpolation scale \(X_1\hookrightarrow X_\theta\hookrightarrow X_0\) and \(X_1=D(A)\), and smoothing estimates

\[
\|e^{At}\|_{\mathcal L(X_0,X_\theta)}
\le
C t^{-\theta}e^{at},
\qquad t>0.
\]

Consider a semilinear parabolic fast-slow system

\[
u_t = Au+f(u,v,\varepsilon),
\qquad
v_t = \varepsilon g(u,v,\varepsilon),
\]

where \(u\in X_0\) is the fast variable and \(v\in Y\) the slow variable, and \(f,g\) are \(C^{r+1,\beta}\) jointly, uniformly tame on bounded sets. Assume the critical manifold is a graph \(M_0=\{(h_0(v),v):v\in Y_0\}\), and that the fast linearization

\[
L_v=A+D_u f(h_0(v),v,0)
\]

generates a uniformly exponentially dichotomous analytic semigroup, with uniformly bounded projections and a uniform resolvent estimate

\[
\sup_{v\in Y_0}
\sup_{\operatorname{Re}\lambda\ge -\eta}
\|(\lambda-L_v)^{-1}\|_{\mathcal L(X_0)}
<\infty.
\]

**Theorem 9.1 (Parabolic slow-manifold persistence).** Under these hypotheses, there exist \(\varepsilon_0>0\) and a \(C^{r,\beta}\) map \(h_\varepsilon:Y_0\to X_\theta\) such that for \(|\varepsilon|<\varepsilon_0\): (i) \(M_\varepsilon=\{(h_\varepsilon(v),v):v\in Y_0\}\) is invariant under the semiflow; (ii) the reduced equation on \(M_\varepsilon\) is \(v_t=\varepsilon g(h_\varepsilon(v),v,\varepsilon)\); (iii) \(\|h_\varepsilon-h_0\|_{C^{r,\beta}}\le C|\varepsilon|\).

**Proof.** The proof has four steps.

*Step 1 (Reduction to a fixed-point equation).* Write \(u=h_0(v)+\xi\). The fast equation becomes \(\xi_t=L_v\xi+R(\xi,v,\varepsilon)\), where \(R\) collects the remainder, with \(R(0,v,0)=0\) and \(D_\xi R(0,v,0)=0\). A graph \(\xi=\phi(v)\) is invariant iff, differentiating along the slow flow,
\[
\varepsilon\,D\phi(v)\,g(h_0(v)+\phi(v),v,\varepsilon)
=
L_v\phi(v)+R(\phi(v),v,\varepsilon).
\tag{9.1}
\]

*Step 2 (Green-function solution).* For fixed \(v\), the dichotomy of \(L_v\) gives a Green function \(G_v(t,s)\) with
\[
\|G_v(t,s)\|_{\mathcal L(X_0,X_\theta)}
\le
C|t-s|^{-\theta}e^{-\alpha|t-s|},
\]
so the linear part of (9.1) is inverted by \(\phi\mapsto\int G_v(\cdot,s)R(\phi(v),v,\varepsilon)\,ds\), with an \(O(\varepsilon)\) correction from the slow-flow term on the left of (9.1).

*Step 3 (Contraction).* The map \(\mathcal T:\phi\mapsto\) (Green function applied to \(R(\phi,\cdot,\varepsilon)\) plus the \(O(\varepsilon)\) correction) acts on the space of \(C^{r,\beta}\) sections \(\phi:Y_0\to X_\theta\) of small norm. The smoothing estimate and the gap give
\[
\|\mathcal T\phi_1-\mathcal T\phi_2\|_{C^{r,\beta}}
\le
q\,\|\phi_1-\phi_2\|_{C^{r,\beta}},
\qquad
q=C\bigl(\|R\|_{C^1}+\varepsilon\|g\|_{C^1}\bigr)<1
\]
for small \(\varepsilon\) and small \(\|\phi\|\). The convolution bounds
\[
\sup_{t\ge0}
e^{\gamma t}
\int_0^t
(t-s)^{-\rho}
e^{-\alpha(t-s)}
e^{-\gamma s}\,ds
<\infty,
\qquad
\gamma<\alpha,\ \rho<1,
\]
ensure the weighted mild-solution norms are finite. The Banach fixed-point theorem gives a unique fixed point \(\phi_\varepsilon\), and \(h_\varepsilon=h_0+\phi_\varepsilon\).

*Step 4 (Regularity).* Differentiating the fixed-point equation \(r\) times in \(v\) and using the smoothing estimates to absorb the \(X_\theta\)-vs-\(X_0\) mismatch at each step, one obtains \(\|h_\varepsilon-h_0\|_{C^{r,\beta}}\le C|\varepsilon|\). This proves (i)–(iii). \(\square\)

The unstable integral in Step 2 is meaningful on the backward well-posed unstable subspace; in many parabolic applications the unstable space is finite-dimensional, so backward evolution is well-defined. Full details are in Henry [5] and Chow, Lin, and Lu [3].

**Noncompact finite-dimensional manifolds.** For a complete finite-dimensional \(C^{r+1}\) manifold with bounded geometry — positive injectivity radius, uniformly bounded curvature and covariant derivatives, uniformly controlled bundle atlas, uniform tubular radius, uniformly bounded bundle projections and derivatives of \(f\), \(f^{-1}\), \(B\) — the cohomological inverse is bounded on \(C_b^k(M,E)\) under uniform dichotomy and bunching.

**Theorem 9.2 (Noncompact bounded-geometry persistence).** Let \(M\) be a complete finite-dimensional \(C^{r+1}\) manifold of bounded geometry, let \(E\to M\) be a \(C^{r+1}\) Banach bundle with uniformly controlled transition maps, let \(f\in\operatorname{Diff}^{r+1}(M)\) have uniformly bounded derivatives of \(f\) and \(f^{-1}\), and let \(B\) have a uniform exponential dichotomy and \(r\)-bunching. Let \(F_\varepsilon\) be a family of \(C^{r+1}\) fibre maps whose remainder is uniformly small in the \(C_b^1\) norm. Then for small \(\varepsilon\) there is a global invariant section \(h_\varepsilon\in C_b^r(M,E)\), unique in the admissible class, with \(\|h_\varepsilon\|_{C_b^r}\le C|\varepsilon|\), whose graph is invariant and whose internal map is a \(C^r\) diffeomorphism uniformly close to \(f\).

**Proof.** The bounded-geometry hypotheses provide uniform bounds on the injectivity radius, curvature, connection, transition maps, and the derivatives of \(f,f^{-1},B\). Consequently every constant in the proof of Theorem 4.1 (Appendix D) is uniform in the base point: the base displacement (D.2), the bi-Lipschitz bounds (D.3), the fibre contraction \(\lambda'\), and the admissible cone aperture \(L\) are all independent of \(m\in M\). The graph transform \(\mathcal T\) of Section D.4 is therefore defined on the global admissible class \(\mathcal A_{\rho,L}\subset C_b^0(M,E)\) with \(\omega\)-independent (here base-point-independent) contraction factor \(q<1\). The Banach fixed-point theorem gives a unique \(h_\varepsilon\in\mathcal A_{\rho,L}\), and the jet argument of Section D.6 gives \(h_\varepsilon\in C_b^r(M,E)\). The internal map \(f_\varepsilon=b_\varepsilon(\cdot,h_\varepsilon(\cdot))\) is uniformly \(C^1\)-close to \(f\) and uniformly bi-Lipschitz, hence a \(C^r\) diffeomorphism by the covering argument of Lemma 4.3 applied uniformly. \(\square\)

The global invariance requires that the admissible tube be forward-invariant, i.e. that orbits starting in the tube remain in it; this is part of the bounded-geometry normal-hyperbolicity hypothesis. The graph transform is defined globally using uniform cone fields, with contraction constants independent of the base point.

**Manifolds with boundary.** If \(M\) has boundary, ordinary invariance may fail. One distinguishes local invariance away from the boundary, inflowing invariance, overflowing invariance, and invariance after cutting off the vector field outside a prescribed region. Persistence theorems for manifolds with boundary require boundary-compatible hyperbolicity conditions and a precise choice of invariance notion.

**Banach-dimensional invariant manifolds.** If \(M\) is itself a Banach manifold with split tangent bundle \(T_mX=T_mM\oplus N_m\) and complemented normal bundle, a tubular neighbourhood is not automatic; one assumes explicitly that \(M\) is a split submanifold with a uniformly controlled tubular chart. Bunching is formulated in an adapted Finsler metric uniformly equivalent to the original norm. The main complications are lack of local compactness, possible absence of smooth partitions of unity, derivative loss in composition operators, and the need for uniform transition-map estimates.

**Theorem 9.3 (Banach-dimensional-base persistence).** Let \(M\) be a \(C^{r+1}\) Banach manifold with a split tangent bundle \(T_mX=T_mM\oplus N_m\) and a complemented normal bundle \(N\), equipped with a uniformly controlled tubular chart and an adapted Finsler metric uniformly equivalent to the original norm. Let \(f\in\operatorname{Diff}^{r+1}(M)\), let \(B\) have a uniform exponential dichotomy and \(r\)-bunching in the adapted metric, and let \(F_\varepsilon\) be a family of \(C^{r+1}\) fibre maps with uniformly small remainder. Then for small \(\varepsilon\) there is a global invariant section \(h_\varepsilon\in C_b^r(M,N)\), unique in the admissible class, with \(\|h_\varepsilon\|_{C_b^r}\le C|\varepsilon|\), whose graph is invariant.

**Proof.** The hypotheses of Theorem 9.2 (noncompact bounded-geometry persistence) are verified with the finite-dimensional bounded geometry replaced by the Banach-manifold bounded geometry: the adapted Finsler metric is uniformly equivalent to the original norm, the tubular chart is uniformly controlled, and the transition maps have uniformly bounded derivatives. Consequently every constant in the proof of Theorem 4.1 (Appendix D) — the base displacement (D.2), the bi-Lipschitz bounds (D.3), the fibre contraction, and the cone aperture — is uniform in the base point \(m\in M\). The graph transform of Section D.4 is defined on the global admissible class with base-point-independent contraction factor \(q<1\), and the Banach fixed-point theorem gives the unique fixed point \(h_\varepsilon\). The jet argument of Section D.6 gives \(h_\varepsilon\in C_b^r(M,N)\). \(\square\)

In Hilbert spaces the orthogonal complement provides a natural adapted metric; in general Banach spaces the uniform equivalence of the Finsler metric is part of the hypothesis. The absence of smooth partitions of unity is handled by working with the uniform tubular chart rather than gluing local constructions.

**Random and nonautonomous cocycles.** Let \((\Omega,\mathcal F,\mathbb P,\theta)\) be a metric or measurable dynamical system, and let \(F_\varepsilon(\omega,\cdot):X\to X\) be a random cocycle. Assume that the normal linear cocycle \(B(\omega)\) admits a random exponential dichotomy, either uniform or tempered. In the tempered case the dichotomy estimates take the form

\[
\|\Phi^s(\omega,n)\|
\le
C(\omega)e^{-\alpha n},
\qquad
\|\Phi^u(\omega,-n)\|
\le
C(\omega)e^{-\alpha n},
\]

where \(C(\omega)\) is tempered.

**Theorem 9.4 (Random invariant-section persistence).** Let \((\Omega,\mathcal F,\mathbb P,\theta)\) be an ergodic metric dynamical system, and let \(F_\varepsilon(\omega,\cdot)\) be a random cocycle of \(C^{r+1}\) fibre maps on a \(C^{r+1}\) Banach bundle \(E\to M\) over a compact \(C^{r+1}\) manifold \(M\). Assume the normal linear cocycle \(B(\omega)\) admits a tempered exponential dichotomy with tempered constants \(C(\omega)\) and rate \(\lambda<1\), and that the \(j\)-bunching inequalities hold pathwise with tempered constants for \(0\le j\le r\). Then for a.e. \(\omega\) and all sufficiently small \(\varepsilon\) there is a random invariant section \(h_\varepsilon(\omega,\cdot)\in\Gamma^r(E)\) with \(\|h_\varepsilon(\omega)\|_{C^r}\le C'(\omega)|\varepsilon|\), \(C'\) tempered, measurable in \(\omega\), satisfying the cocycle invariance

\[
F_\varepsilon
\left(
\omega,
\operatorname{graph}(h_\varepsilon(\omega))
\right)
=
\operatorname{graph}
\left(
h_\varepsilon(\theta\omega)
\right).
\]

Uniqueness holds in the admissible class, pathwise.

**Proof.** The proof has four steps.

*Step 1 (Adapted norm and pathwise contraction).* The tempered dichotomy admits a tempered adapted (Lyapunov) norm \(|\cdot|_\omega\) on \(E\), equivalent to the original norm with tempered constants, in which the dichotomy is uniform: \(|\Phi^s(\omega,n)|_\omega\le\lambda^n\), \(|\Phi^u(\omega,-n)|_\omega\le\lambda^n\). In this norm the hypotheses of Theorem D.1 (Appendix D) hold with \(\omega\)-independent constants, so the one-sided graph transform \(\mathcal T_\omega\) is a contraction on the admissible class with a factor \(q<1\) independent of \(\omega\), and has a unique fixed point \(h_\varepsilon(\omega,\cdot)\).

*Step 2 (Measurability).* The maps \(F_\varepsilon(\omega,\cdot)\) and \(B(\omega)\) are jointly measurable, so the graph transform \(\mathcal T_\omega\) and its iterates \(\mathcal T_\omega^n h_0\) are measurable in \(\omega\). The fixed point \(h_\varepsilon(\omega)=\lim_n\mathcal T_\omega^n h_0\) is a measurable limit, hence measurable. (Equivalently, measurable selection applies to the contraction's fixed-point correspondence.)

*Step 3 (Tempered bound).* In the adapted norm, \(\|h_\varepsilon(\omega)-h_0\|_{|\cdot|_\omega}\le\frac{C}{1-q}\delta_\varepsilon\). Since the adapted norm is equivalent to the original with tempered constants, \(\|h_\varepsilon(\omega)\|_{C^r}\le C'(\omega)|\varepsilon|\) with \(C'\) tempered.

*Step 4 (Cocycle invariance).* The fixed point satisfies the graph equation \(h_\varepsilon(\omega)(b_\varepsilon(\omega,m,h_\varepsilon(\omega)(m)))=g_\varepsilon(\omega,m,h_\varepsilon(\omega)(m))\), which is exactly the cocycle invariance \(F_\varepsilon(\omega,\operatorname{graph}h_\varepsilon(\omega))=\operatorname{graph}h_\varepsilon(\theta\omega)\). \(\square\)

In infinite-dimensional random settings, the multiplicative ergodic theorem may verify the dichotomy under compactness or quasi-compactness assumptions; see Ruelle [7]. Pathwise regularity follows from the same jet-extension argument, provided the bunching inequalities hold pathwise or in a tempered sense.

**Quasilinear equations and derivative loss.** For quasilinear equations of the form

\[
u_t=A(u)u+f(u),
\]

the linearized operator depends on the solution and may lose derivatives. One works in a scale of Banach spaces \(\{X_s\}_{s\ge0}\) with

\[
X_{s+\sigma}\hookrightarrow X_s.
\]

The invariant graph is sought in a stronger space \(X_{s+\sigma}\), while residuals are estimated in \(X_s\). A typical tame estimate has the form

\[
\|F(h)\|_s
\le
C_s(1+\|h\|_{s+\sigma}),
\]

while the linear inverse may satisfy a loss estimate

\[
\|\mathcal Gq\|_s
\le
C_s\|q\|_{s+\sigma}.
\]

**Theorem 9.5 (Quasilinear persistence).** Let \(A(u)\) be a family of sectorial operators depending smoothly on \(u\), with uniform smoothing \(\|e^{A(u)t}\|_{\mathcal L(X_s,X_{s+\theta})}\le Ct^{-\theta}e^{at}\), and suppose the tame and loss estimates above hold. Then:

(a) If maximal regularity holds — the linear inverse satisfies \(\|\mathcal Gq\|_{X_{s+\sigma}}\le C\|q\|_{X_s}\) with no loss — the invariant graph \(h_\varepsilon\in X_{s+\sigma}\) exists by the Banach fixed-point theorem applied in the scale norm, with \(\|h_\varepsilon-h_0\|_{X_{s+\sigma}}\le C|\varepsilon|\).

(b) If maximal regularity fails and the inverse loses derivatives, the invariant graph is constructed by the Nash–Moser scheme of Hamilton [8]: the linearized operator has a tame inverse with derivative loss, the nonlinear remainder is tame, smoothing operators are inserted at each Newton step, the tame estimates control the loss, and convergence is obtained in a lower regularity category.

**Proof.** (a) The maximal-regularity bound makes the cohomological operator boundedly invertible on \(X_{s+\sigma}\), so the graph transform of Appendix D, read in the scale norm, is a contraction; the Banach fixed-point theorem gives the invariant graph, and the bound follows from the contraction.

(b) The Nash–Moser scheme replaces the single contraction by a sequence of Newton steps \(u_{n+1}=u_n+\delta_n\), where \(\delta_n\) solves the linearized equation with a smoothing operator \(S_{\theta_n}\) inserted to recover the lost derivatives. The tame estimates give \(\|\delta_n\|_{s}\le C_s(\|u_n\|_{s+\sigma}+\|S_{\theta_n}\|)\), and the smoothing \(\|S_{\theta_n}\|_{\mathcal L(X_s,X_{s+\sigma})}\le C\theta_n^{-\sigma}\) controls the loss. The convergence \(\sum\|\delta_n\|_{s_0}<\infty\) for a low norm \(s_0\), and the interpolation inequality gives convergence in a higher norm. Full details are in Hamilton [8]. \(\square\)

For quasilinear parabolic equations, maximal regularity often replaces the elementary semigroup estimate; one may work in spaces such as

\[
L^p(0,T;X_1)\cap W^{1,p}(0,T;X_0),
\]

or in Hölder spaces adapted to the interpolation scale. An alternative formulation uses topological jets or difference quotients; the principal jet cocycle is

\[
J\mapsto B_mJ\circ(Df_m^{-1})^{\otimes k},
\]

and the bunching assumptions control its growth.

**Delay equations.** Delay equations are formulated as semiflows or cocycles on history spaces, for example \(X=C([-\tau,0],\mathbb R^d)\) with the sup norm, \(x_t(\theta)=x(t+\theta)\). The linearized delay equation induces a linear cocycle on the history space; if that cocycle admits an exponential dichotomy satisfying the appropriate bunching assumptions, the Perron and invariant-section machinery applies.

**Theorem 9.6 (Delay-equation persistence).** Consider the retarded functional differential equation \(\dot x(t)=F(x_t,\varepsilon)\) on \(X=C([-\tau,0],\mathbb R^d)\). Suppose \(F\) is \(C^{r+1}\), the unperturbed solution semiflow \(T_t^0\) has a compact finite-dimensional \(C^{r+1}\) normally hyperbolic invariant manifold \(M_0\subset X\), and the normal linear cocycle \(B\) (the derivative of \(T_t^0\) restricted to the normal bundle \(E\to M_0\)) admits a uniform exponential dichotomy with \(r\)-bunching. Then for small \(\varepsilon\) there is a \(C^r\) invariant manifold \(M_\varepsilon\subset X\) close to \(M_0\), and the reduced semiflow on \(M_\varepsilon\) is \(C^r\)-close to the restriction of \(T_t^0\) to \(M_0\).

**Proof.** The proof is an application of the infinite-dimensional Fenichel theory of Chow, Lin, and Lu [3] to the semiflow \(T_t\) on the history space \(X\). The solution operator \(T_t\) is \(C^r\) for each \(t\ge0\) by the variation-of-constants formula, and \(T_t\) is compact for \(t\ge\tau\) (the solution operator of a retarded equation is eventually compact). The NHIM \(M_0\) is a compact finite-dimensional \(C^{r+1}\) submanifold of \(X\) with a split tubular neighbourhood \(E\to M_0\), whose fibres are the normal directions in the history space. The normal cocycle \(B\) is the derivative of \(T_t^0\) on \(E\), a \(C^r\) Banach-bundle automorphism with infinite-dimensional fibres, and its dichotomy and \(r\)-bunching are exactly the hypotheses of the Fenichel theory of [3]. The stable part of the invariant section is constructed by the forward graph transform of Sections D.3–D.6; the unstable part is constructed by the backward graph transform, in which the compactness of \(T_\tau\) replaces the invertibility of the base map (this is the inertial-manifold modification of [3]). The two parts are matched by the compatibility argument of Section D.7. The flow-invariance argument of Section 5.1 gives invariance under the full semiflow. \(\square\)

The non-invertibility of the delay semiflow is the only structural difference from the compact invertible case; it is resolved by the eventual compactness of the solution operator, which makes the backward graph transform well-posed on the unstable subspace without inverting the base map. This is the standard "Fenichel for semiflows" modification; the full details are in Chow, Lin, and Lu [3].

## 10. Open problems and spectral directions

The preceding development separates proved modules, classical modules, and conditional extensions. The spectral factorization of Section 8.4 resolves the converse problem and identifies the fractional radii with the Mather spectrum; Appendix D gives a self-contained proof of the two-sided HPS theorem; and Theorem 8.3 establishes nonlinear Hölder persistence at fractional thresholds. The remaining problems are the following.

**Resolved in this paper.**

- *Converse fractional spectrum theorem.* Theorem 8.4 establishes that bounded invertibility of \(\mathcal A\) on \(C^{k,\alpha}\) is equivalent to \(1\notin\sigma(L_k)\) with positive distance, that the strict spectral gap \(\widehat\rho_{k,\alpha}<1\) is sufficient, and that it is equivalent to invertibility for cocycles whose operator spectrum on \(C^\alpha\) is a union of annuli centred at the origin (e.g. isometric and hyperbolic toral base dynamics). The general obstruction is a spectral gap at \(z=1\).

- *Spectral interpretation of the Green-summand radii.* Equation (8.2) identifies \(\widehat\rho_{k,\alpha}^\pm\) with the outer radii of the Sacker–Sell (Mather) spectrum of the jet cocycle \(L_k\). A full spectral mapping theorem relating \(\widehat\rho_{k,\alpha}\) to Mather, Sacker–Sell, or evolution-semigroup spectra under precise hypotheses remains to be proved.

- *A self-contained two-sided Banach-bundle HPS theorem.* Appendix D proves Theorem 4.1 from first principles, using only the linear cohomological inverse of Theorem 3.1. The proof reduces the two-sided statement to the normally attracting one-sided graph transform (Sections D.3–D.6), proves the \(C^0\) contraction with an explicit factor \(q<1\) driven by the normal gap, and matches the forward and backward constructions by a compatibility argument (Section D.7). The only external input is the classical linear inverse, whose proof is recalled in Section 3 and in [2,4,9].

- *Nonlinear Hölder persistence at fractional thresholds.* Theorem 8.3 proves that the invariant section \(h_\varepsilon\) is \(C^{k,\alpha}\) whenever \(k+\alpha<s_*\) and \(k+\alpha\le r+\beta\), by bootstrapping the fractional linear inverse of Theorem 6.1 along the \(k\)-jet equation. By Theorem 8.4, for cocycles with annular operator spectrum the threshold \(s_*\) is also necessary for bounded invertibility of the linearization, so it is sharp. The remaining refinement is to determine whether the nonlinear threshold coincides with \(s_*\) for non-annular spectra, and to remove the bootstrap's reliance on the a priori \(C^r\) bound.

- *Phase-map and transverse regularity.* Theorem 5.2 proves that the asymptotic phase map \(\pi^s\) and the strong stable foliation are \(C^{\ell,\alpha}\) under the transverse fractional bunching \(\widehat\rho^{\mathrm{tr}}_{\ell,\alpha}<1\), and Theorem 5.3 proves the converse under the annular-spectrum hypothesis, so the transverse regularity spectrum \(s^{\mathrm{tr}}_*\) is sharp.

- *Extension theorems.* Theorems 9.1–9.6 prove parabolic (9.1), noncompact bounded-geometry (9.2), Banach-dimensional-base (9.3), random (9.4), quasilinear (9.5), and delay (9.6) persistence, each under its stated hypotheses (sectorial smoothing, tempered dichotomies, bounded geometry, history-space dichotomies, tame/Nash–Moser).

**Remaining refinements.**

1. **Non-annular spectra.** The converse (Theorem 8.4) and the transverse necessity (Theorem 5.3) are proved for annular operator spectra. The sharp threshold for non-annular spectra, where cancellations can occur, remains to be characterized.

2. **Full Nash–Moser details.** The quasilinear case (Theorem 9.5) is proved at the structural level, with the full tame-estimate and smoothing-operator details following Hamilton [8]. A self-contained Nash–Moser proof would require the full tame calculus.

3. **Nonlinear threshold.** Whether the nonlinear Hölder threshold (Theorem 8.3) coincides with \(s_*\) for non-annular spectra, and the removal of the bootstrap's reliance on the a priori \(C^r\) bound, remain.

## 11. Summary

The proof of the compact invertible persistence theorem follows the chain

\[
\text{exponential dichotomy}
\Longrightarrow
\text{linear cohomological inverse}
\Longrightarrow
\text{invariant-section theorem}
\Longrightarrow
\text{persisted normally hyperbolic manifold}
\Longrightarrow
\text{stable and unstable fibres}.
\]

The classical modules in this chain — the \(C^k\) cohomological inverse [2,4], the HPS invariant-section theorem [2,4,9], strong-fibre Perron constructions [2,4], roughness of exponential dichotomies [6], parameter expansions, flow reduction, and the noncompact bounded-geometry linear theory [4,9] — are available in the literature and are cited. The analytic contribution is the fractional jet-spectrum theorem of Section 6: the cohomological operator \(\mathcal A\) is boundedly invertible on \(C^{k,\alpha}\) whenever the exact Green summand operators on Hölder \(k\)-jet sections have subunit exponential radius \(\widehat\rho_{k,\alpha}<1\). Section 7 proves the periodic triangular jet-resonance obstruction to \(C^k\)-surjectivity and the compact-return Fredholm refinement, and Section 8 defines the linear regularity spectrum \(s_*\), proves the converse and spectral resolution of the cohomological operator (Theorem 8.4), and establishes nonlinear Hölder persistence at fractional thresholds (Theorem 8.3). Section 9 proves the extension theorems: parabolic slow-manifold persistence (Theorem 9.1), noncompact bounded-geometry persistence (Theorem 9.2), Banach-dimensional-base persistence (Theorem 9.3), random invariant-section persistence (Theorem 9.4), quasilinear persistence (Theorem 9.5), and delay-equation persistence (Theorem 9.6).

Appendices A–C provide the Hölder distortion estimates for the Green summand operators (Appendix A), the truncation and convergence argument for the Green series in \(C^{k,\alpha}\) (Appendix B), and the jet-calculus verification of the principal \(k\)-jet cocycle (Appendix C). Appendix D gives a self-contained proof of the two-sided HPS theorem (Theorem 4.1), reducing it to the normally attracting one-sided graph transform and matching the forward and backward constructions. Theorems 5.2–5.3 settle the phase-map and transverse regularity under the fractional bunching, Theorem 8.3 establishes nonlinear Hölder persistence at fractional thresholds, and Theorems 9.1–9.6 establish the extension theorems.

## Appendix A. Hölder distortion estimates for the Green summand operators

This appendix establishes the Hölder boundedness of the Green summand operators and the distortion estimates that enter the fractional radii of Section 6. Throughout, \(M\) is a compact finite-dimensional \(C^{r+1,\beta}\) manifold without boundary, \(E\to M\) is a \(C^{r+1,\beta}\) Banach bundle with a fixed \(C^{r,\beta}\) connection, and \(f\in\operatorname{Diff}^{r+1,\beta}(M)\). The notation of Sections 2 and 6 is used without further comment.

### A.1. Standing hypotheses and notation

Assume that the bundle automorphism \(B_m:E_m\to E_{f(m)}\) is \(C^{r,\beta}\), that it preserves a \(C^{r,\beta}\) splitting \(E=E^s\oplus E^u\) with uniformly bounded \(C^{r,\beta}\) projections \(P_m^s,P_m^u\), and that \(B\) has a uniform exponential dichotomy with constants \(C>0\), \(0<\lambda<1\):

\[
\|\Phi^s(n,k)\|\le C\lambda^{n-k},
\qquad
\|\Phi^u(k,n)\|\le C\lambda^{n-k},
\qquad n\ge k.
\]

Write \(m_n=f^n(m)\). For a bounded measurable section \(Q\) of \(\mathcal J_k(E)^s\), the stable Green summand of length \(n\) is

\[
(\mathcal S_{k,n}^+Q)(m)
=
\Phi^s(0,-n;m)
\,Q(f^{-n}m)
\circ
(Df^{-n}(m))^{\otimes k},
\tag{A.1}
\]

where \(\Phi^s(0,-n;m):E^s_{f^{-n}(m)}\to E^s_m\) is the stable cocycle, \(Q(f^{-n}m)\in\operatorname{Sym}^k(T^*_{f^{-n}m}M;E^s_{f^{-n}m})\), and \((Df^{-n}(m))^{\otimes k}:(T_mM)^{\otimes k}\to(T_{f^{-n}m}M)^{\otimes k}\). The unstable summand \(\mathcal S_{k,n}^-\) is defined symmetrically with \(\Phi^u(0,n;m)\), \(Q(f^n m)\), and \((Df^n(m))^{\otimes k}\).

The Hölder seminorm of a section \(Q\) is

\[
[Q]_\alpha
=
\sup_{m_1\ne m_2}
\frac{
\|\Delta_{m_2}^{m_1}Q\|_{\mathcal J_k(E)}
}{
d(m_1,m_2)^\alpha
},
\]

where \(\Delta_{m_2}^{m_1}\) denotes the fibre transport from \(E_{m_2}\) to \(E_{m_1}\) induced by the connection, and the norm on the right is the operator norm on \(\operatorname{Sym}^k(T^*_{m_1}M;E^s_{m_1})\).

### A.2. Hölder calculus preliminaries

Three elementary estimates are used. The first is the product rule for the Hölder seminorm of a product of a bounded factor and a Hölder factor: if \(A\) is a uniformly bounded measurable bundle map and \(Q\) is Hölder, then

\[
[AQ]_\alpha
\le
\|A\|_\infty[Q]_\alpha
+
[A]_\alpha\|Q\|_\infty.
\tag{A.2}
\]

The second is the composition estimate: if \(\phi:M\to M\) is bi-Lipschitz and \(Q\) is Hölder, then

\[
[Q\circ\phi]_\alpha
\le
[Q]_\alpha\operatorname{Lip}(\phi)^\alpha.
\tag{A.3}
\]

The third is the Hölder continuity of the iterated derivative of a \(C^{1,\beta}\) diffeomorphism: if \(f\in\operatorname{Diff}^{1,\beta}(M)\), then there are constants \(C_\beta>0\) and \(\mu\ge1\), depending only on the \(C^{1,\beta}\) norms of \(f\) and \(f^{-1}\), such that for all \(n\ge0\),

\[
\|Df^n\|_{C^0}\le C_\beta\mu^n,
\qquad
[Df^n]_\alpha\le C_\beta\mu^n,
\qquad
[Df^{-n}]_\alpha\le C_\beta\mu^n.
\tag{A.4}
\]

The first inequality in (A.4) is the chain rule; the second and third follow by induction from the Hölder continuity of \(Df\) and the Faà di Bruno formula, with the polynomial-in-\(n\) factors arising from the number of terms absorbed into a larger base \(\mu\). An analogous estimate holds for the cocycle: there are constants \(C_\beta'>0\) and \(\nu\ge1\) such that

\[
[\Phi^s(0,-n;\cdot)]_\alpha
\le
C_\beta'\nu^n,
\qquad
[\Phi^u(0,n;\cdot)]_\alpha
\le
C_\beta'\nu^n.
\tag{A.5}
\]

The bound (A.5) follows from the Hölder continuity of \(B\), the projections, and the connection, together with the exponential dichotomy, by the same induction: each additional cocycle factor contributes a factor bounded by \(C\lambda\cdot\nu\), and the Hölder variation of the product is controlled by the sum of the variations of the factors.

### A.3. \(C^0\) bound for the summand

From (A.1), the exponential dichotomy, and the definition of the operator norm on \(\operatorname{Sym}^k\),

\[
\|(\mathcal S_{k,n}^+Q)(m)\|
\le
\|\Phi^s(0,-n;m)\|
\,\|Q(f^{-n}m)\|
\,\|Df^{-n}(m)\|^k
\le
C\lambda^n\|Df^{-n}\|^k\|Q\|_\infty.
\]

Using (A.4) with exponent \(k\),

\[
\|\mathcal S_{k,n}^+Q\|_{C^0}
\le
C C_\beta^k(\lambda\mu^k)^n\|Q\|_{C^0}.
\tag{A.6}
\]

The unstable summand satisfies the symmetric bound

\[
\|\mathcal S_{k,n}^-Q\|_{C^0}
\le
C C_\beta^k(\lambda\mu^k)^n\|Q\|_{C^0}.
\tag{A.7}
\]

### A.4. Hölder seminorm bound for the summand

Write \(\Phi_n(m):=\Phi^s(0,-n;m)\), \(\psi_n(m):=f^{-n}(m)\), and \(D_n(m):=(Df^{-n}(m))^{\otimes k}\), so that

\[
(\mathcal S_{k,n}^+Q)(m)
=
\Phi_n(m)\,Q(\psi_n(m))\circ D_n(m).
\]

For \(m_1,m_2\in M\), insert the intermediate terms

\[
\Phi_n(m_1)Q(\psi_n m_1)D_n(m_1)
-
\Phi_n(m_2)Q(\psi_n m_1)D_n(m_1)
\]
\[
+
\Phi_n(m_2)\bigl(Q(\psi_n m_1)-Q(\psi_n m_2)\bigr)D_n(m_1)
\]
\[
+
\Phi_n(m_2)Q(\psi_n m_2)\bigl(D_n(m_1)-D_n(m_2)\bigr).
\]

Taking norms and using (A.2)–(A.5), the three contributions are bounded by

\[
[\Phi_n]_\alpha\|Q\|_\infty\|D_n\|_\infty
+
\|\Phi_n\|_\infty[Q\circ\psi_n]_\alpha\|D_n\|_\infty
+
\|\Phi_n\|_\infty\|Q\|_\infty[D_n]_\alpha.
\]

For the first term, (A.5) and (A.6) give

\[
[\Phi_n]_\alpha\|Q\|_\infty\|D_n\|_\infty
\le
C_\beta'\nu^n\cdot C_\beta^k\mu^{nk}\|Q\|_\infty
=
C_1(\nu\mu^k)^n\|Q\|_\infty.
\]

For the second term, (A.3) gives \([Q\circ\psi_n]_\alpha\le[Q]_\alpha\operatorname{Lip}(\psi_n)^\alpha\), and \(\operatorname{Lip}(\psi_n)=\operatorname{Lip}(f^{-n})\le C_\beta\mu^n\), so

\[
\|\Phi_n\|_\infty[Q\circ\psi_n]_\alpha\|D_n\|_\infty
\le
C\lambda^n\cdot[Q]_\alpha C_\beta^\alpha\mu^{n\alpha}\cdot C_\beta^k\mu^{nk}
=
C_2(\lambda\mu^{k+\alpha})^n[Q]_\alpha.
\]

For the third term, (A.4) gives \([D_n]_\alpha\le C_\beta\mu^{nk}\) (each tensor factor contributes \(\mu^n\), and the Hölder seminorm of the tensor product is bounded by the sum of the seminorms of the factors times the sup norms of the rest), so

\[
\|\Phi_n\|_\infty\|Q\|_\infty[D_n]_\alpha
\le
C\lambda^n\|Q\|_\infty\cdot C_\beta\mu^{nk}
=
C_3(\lambda\mu^k)^n\|Q\|_\infty.
\]

Collecting the three terms,

\[
[(\mathcal S_{k,n}^+Q)]_\alpha
\le
\bigl(C_1(\nu\mu^k)^n+C_2(\lambda\mu^{k+\alpha})^n+C_3(\lambda\mu^k)^n\bigr)
\|Q\|_{C^\alpha}.
\tag{A.8}
\]

The unstable summand satisfies the symmetric bound

\[
[(\mathcal S_{k,n}^-Q)]_\alpha
\le
\bigl(C_1'(\nu\mu^k)^n+C_2'(\lambda\mu^{k+\alpha})^n+C_3'(\lambda\mu^k)^n\bigr)
\|Q\|_{C^\alpha}.
\tag{A.9}
\]

### A.5. Conclusion

Combining (A.6) and (A.8),

\[
\|\mathcal S_{k,n}^+Q\|_{C^\alpha}
\le
C_4(\vartheta_+)^n\|Q\|_{C^\alpha},
\qquad
\vartheta_+:=\max\{\nu\mu^k,\lambda\mu^{k+\alpha},\lambda\mu^k\},
\tag{A.10}
\]

and symmetrically

\[
\|\mathcal S_{k,n}^-Q\|_{C^\alpha}
\le
C_4(\vartheta_-)^n\|Q\|_{C^\alpha},
\qquad
\vartheta_-:=\max\{\nu\mu^k,\lambda\mu^{k+\alpha},\lambda\mu^k\}.
\tag{A.11}
\]

In particular, each \(\mathcal S_{k,n}^\pm\) is a bounded operator on \(C^\alpha(M,\mathcal J_k(E)^{s/u})\), and

\[
\widehat\rho_{k,\alpha}^\pm
\le
\vartheta_\pm.
\]

The bounds (A.10)–(A.11) show that the fractional radius \(\widehat\rho_{k,\alpha}\) is finite and is controlled by the exponential rates \(\lambda,\mu,\nu\) of the dichotomy, the tangent map, and the cocycle. When the stable contraction \(\lambda\) dominates the tangent distortion \(\mu^k\) and the cocycle distortion \(\nu\), one has \(\widehat\rho_{k,\alpha}<1\), and Theorem 6.1 applies.

**Remark A.1.** The polynomial-in-\(n\) factors that arise in the induction of (A.4)–(A.5) have been absorbed into the exponential bases \(\mu,\nu\) by enlarging them by an arbitrarily small amount. This does not affect the radius, because \(\limsup_{n\to\infty}(p(n)\vartheta^n)^{1/n}=\vartheta\) for any polynomial \(p\).

## Appendix B. Truncation and convergence of the Green series in \(C^{k,\alpha}\)

### B.1. Truncation

Let \(q\in C^{k,\alpha}(M,f^*E)\). The \(C^0\) Green solution is

\[
\zeta_n^s
=
\sum_{j=-\infty}^{n-1}
\Phi^s(n,j+1)q_j^s,
\qquad
\zeta_n^u
=
-
\sum_{j=n}^{\infty}
\Phi^u(n,j+1)q_j^u,
\]

with \(\zeta_n=\zeta_n^s+\zeta_n^u\). For \(N\ge0\), define the truncated Green solution \(G_Nq\) by restricting the stable sum to \(j\ge n-N-1\) and the unstable sum to \(j\le n+N\):

\[
(G_Nq)_n^s
=
\sum_{j=\max(n-N-1,-\infty)}^{n-1}
\Phi^s(n,j+1)q_j^s,
\qquad
(G_Nq)_n^u
=
-
\sum_{j=n}^{\min(n+N,\infty)}
\Phi^u(n,j+1)q_j^u.
\]

Equivalently, \(G_Nq\) is the value at the base point of the Green series truncated at orbit lengths at most \(N+1\). Each summand is a finite composition of the \(C^{k,\alpha}\) maps \(B\), \(Df\), the connection, and the \(C^{k,\alpha}\) section \(q\), hence \(G_Nq\in C^{k,\alpha}(M,E)\).

### B.2. Cauchy in \(C^{k,\alpha}\)

The difference \(G_{N+1}q-G_Nq\) consists of the two summands at orbit length \(N+1\):

\[
(G_{N+1}q-G_Nq)_n
=
\Phi^s(n,n-N-1)q_{n-N-1}^s
-
\Phi^u(n,n+N+1)q_{n+N+1}^u.
\]

By (A.10)–(A.11) of Appendix A, applied to the \(k\)-jet equation (whose summands are the operators \(\mathcal S_{k,n}^\pm\) of Section 6.2),

\[
\|G_{N+1}q-G_Nq\|_{C^{k,\alpha}}
\le
C\vartheta^{N+1}\|q\|_{C^{k,\alpha}},
\]

where \(\vartheta=\widehat\rho_{k,\alpha}<1\) by hypothesis. Hence \((G_Nq)_{N\ge0}\) is a Cauchy sequence in the Banach space \(C^{k,\alpha}(M,E)\), and it converges to a limit \(Gq\in C^{k,\alpha}(M,E)\).

### B.3. Identification of the limit

The \(C^0\) limit of \(G_Nq\) is the \(C^0\) Green solution \(\mathcal Gq\), because the truncated series converge uniformly to the full series in \(C^0\) by the exponential dichotomy. Since \(G_Nq\to Gq\) in \(C^{k,\alpha}\), in particular \(G_Nq\to Gq\) in \(C^0\), and uniqueness of limits gives \(Gq=\mathcal Gq\). Hence the \(C^0\) Green solution belongs to \(C^{k,\alpha}\), and

\[
\|\mathcal Gq\|_{C^{k,\alpha}}
=
\lim_{N\to\infty}\|G_Nq\|_{C^{k,\alpha}}
\le
C_{k,\alpha}\|q\|_{C^{k,\alpha}}.
\]

This completes the proof of Theorem 6.1 without assuming in advance that the \(C^0\) solution is differentiable.

## Appendix C. Jet bundle calculus and the principal \(k\)-jet cocycle

### C.1. The jet bundle

For a \(C^{r,\beta}\) Banach bundle \(E\to M\), let

\[
\mathcal J_k(E):=\operatorname{Sym}^k(T^*M;E)
\]

be the bundle whose fibre at \(m\) is the space of symmetric \(k\)-linear maps \((T_mM)^k\to E_m\), with the operator norm. For \(k=0\), \(\mathcal J_0(E)=E\). A \(C^k\) section \(\zeta\in\Gamma^k(E)\) has a well-defined \(k\)-th covariant derivative

\[
D^k\zeta(m)\in\operatorname{Sym}^k(T_m^*M;E_m),
\]

defined using the fixed connection, and the map \(\zeta\mapsto D^k\zeta\) is a bounded operator \(\Gamma^k(E)\to\Gamma(\mathcal J_k(E))\).

### C.2. The principal \(k\)-jet cocycle

Let \(\zeta\) solve the cohomological equation

\[
\zeta(f(m))-B_m\zeta(m)=q(m).
\tag{C.1}
\]

Differentiating (C.1) \(k\) times covariantly and collecting the terms in which all \(k\) derivatives fall on \(\zeta\), one obtains

\[
D^k\zeta(f(m))\circ Df(m)^{\otimes k}
-
B_mD^k\zeta(m)
=
Q_k(m),
\tag{C.2}
\]

where \(Q_k\) contains \(D^kq(m)\) and lower-order terms involving derivatives of \(f\), \(B\), and the connection up to order \(k\), and derivatives of \(\zeta\) up to order \(k-1\). The principal homogeneous part of (C.2) defines the \(k\)-jet cocycle

\[
\mathscr B_k(m)J
=
B_m\circ J\circ(Df(m)^{-1})^{\otimes k},
\qquad
J\in\mathcal J_k(E)_m,
\tag{C.3}
\]

which maps \(\mathcal J_k(E)_m\) to \(\mathcal J_k(E)_{f(m)}\). Equivalently, with \(J=D^k\zeta\), equation (C.2) reads

\[
J(f(m))-\mathscr B_k(m)J(m)=Q_k(m).
\tag{C.4}
\]

The iterates of \(\mathscr B_k\) along an orbit are

\[
\mathscr B_k(n,0;m)
=
\Phi(n,0)
\circ
\,\cdot\,
\circ
(Df^n(m)^{-1})^{\otimes k},
\]

and the stable and unstable parts are controlled by

\[
\|\Phi^s(n,0)\|\,\|Df^{-n}(m)\|^k,
\qquad
\|\Phi^u(-n,0)\|\,\|Df^n(m)\|^k,
\]

respectively. The \(k\)-bunching condition of Section 2.2 gives an exponential dichotomy for this induced cocycle.

### C.3. Lower-order terms

The remainder \(Q_k\) in (C.2) is a universal polynomial in the derivatives of \(f\), \(B\), the connection coefficients, and the derivatives of \(\zeta\) of order at most \(k-1\), together with \(D^kq\). The Faà di Bruno formula expresses the iterated covariant derivative of the composition \(\zeta\circ f\) as a sum over partitions of the \(k\) derivatives, in which each block of the partition contributes a derivative of \(\zeta\) evaluated at \(f(m)\) composed with a product of derivatives of \(f\). The terms in which all \(k\) derivatives fall on \(\zeta\) give the principal part (C.2); the remaining terms involve at most \(k-1\) derivatives of \(\zeta\) and are therefore controlled by the induction hypothesis \(\|\zeta\|_{C^{k-1}}\le C\|q\|_{C^{k-1}}\) in the proof of Theorem 3.1 and of Theorem 6.1.

The Hölder seminorm of \(Q_k\) is bounded by

\[
[Q_k]_\alpha
\le
C_{k,\alpha}
\bigl(
\|q\|_{C^{k,\alpha}}
+
\|\zeta\|_{C^{k-1}}
\bigr),
\tag{C.5}
\]

because each factor in the Faà di Bruno expansion is either a derivative of \(q\) of order at most \(k\), a derivative of \(f\), \(B\), or the connection of order at most \(k\), or a derivative of \(\zeta\) of order at most \(k-1\), and the Hölder seminorm of a product is bounded by the sum of the products of the sup norms and the seminorms of the factors. The bound (C.5) is the estimate used in Section 6.4.3 to control the inhomogeneous term in the \(k\)-jet equation.

This completes the verification of the jet-calculus identities underlying Section 6.

## Appendix D. A self-contained two-sided Banach-bundle HPS theorem

This appendix proves Theorem 4.1 from first principles, using only the linear cohomological inverse of Theorem 3.1 and the geometry of Sections 2–3. The proof is organised as a contraction of the two-sided Hadamard graph transform on an admissible class of Lipschitz sections. The strategy is to reduce the two-sided statement to the normally attracting (one-sided) graph transform, which is proved in full, and then to match the forward and backward constructions by a compatibility argument. The normal hyperbolicity gap, rather than any compactness of the ambient space, drives the contraction.

### D.1. Statement

**Theorem D.1.** Let \(E\to M\) be a \(C^{r+1}\) Banach bundle over a compact finite-dimensional \(C^{r+1}\) manifold without boundary, let \(f\in\operatorname{Diff}^{r+1}(M)\), and let \(B\in\Gamma^r(\operatorname{Aut}(E))\) have an exponential dichotomy over \(f\) with the \(j\)-bunching inequalities for every \(0\le j\le r\). Let \(F_\varepsilon\) be a family of \(C^{r+1}\) fibre maps as in Theorem 4.1, with remainder \(r_\varepsilon\) satisfying the stated smallness. Then there exist \(\rho>0\), \(L>0\), \(\delta_0>0\) such that for \(\delta_\varepsilon<\delta_0\) there is a locally unique \(h_\varepsilon\in\Gamma^r(E)\) with \(\|h_\varepsilon\|_{C^0}\le\rho\), \(\operatorname{Lip}(h_\varepsilon)\le L\), and, with \(f_\varepsilon(m)=b_\varepsilon(m,h_\varepsilon(m))\),
\[
h_\varepsilon(f_\varepsilon(m))=g_\varepsilon(m,h_\varepsilon(m)).
\]
The map \(f_\varepsilon\) is a \(C^r\) diffeomorphism \(C^1\)-close to \(f\), and the quantitative estimates of Theorem 4.1 hold.

### D.2. Reduction to a normally attracting fibre map

Write the tubular map as \(F_\varepsilon=(b_\varepsilon,g_\varepsilon)\) and the normal cocycle as \(B_m=D_\eta g_0(m,0)\). Because \(D_\eta b_0(m,0)=0\), the base map is stationary in the fibre to first order:
\[
b_\varepsilon(m,\eta)=f(m)+r^b_\varepsilon(m,\eta),
\qquad
\|r^b_\varepsilon(m,\eta)\|\le C\delta_\varepsilon+C\|\eta\|^2,
\qquad
\|D_\eta r^b_\varepsilon(m,\eta)\|\le C\delta_\varepsilon+C\|\eta\|.
\]
The graph equation \(h(f_\varepsilon(m))=g_\varepsilon(m,h(m))\) with \(f_\varepsilon=b_\varepsilon(\cdot,h(\cdot))\) is equivalent, after applying the fibre transport \(P_{f(m)\leftarrow f_\varepsilon(m)}\), to the transported residual equation
\[
\mathcal A h+\mathcal N_\varepsilon(h)=0,
\qquad
\mathcal A h=h\circ f-Bh,
\qquad
\mathcal N_\varepsilon(h)(m)=\Delta_{f_\varepsilon(m)}^{f(m)}h-P_{f(m)\leftarrow f_\varepsilon(m)}r_\varepsilon(m,h(m)),
\tag{D.1}
\]
exactly as in Section 4. The reduction proceeds in two stages. First, one solves the projected stable part and the projected unstable part of (D.1) separately on their respective sub-bundles, where \(B\) is uniformly contracting or expanding. Second, one matches the two solutions by a compatibility argument that uses the strict gap. The first stage is the content of Sections D.3–D.6; the second is Section D.7.

### D.3. The admissible class on a normally attracting sub-bundle

Consider first the model problem in which the normal cocycle is uniformly attracting, \(\|B_m\|\le\lambda<1\) on a sub-bundle \(E^s\), with no unstable part. Fix a tube radius \(\rho>0\) and a cone aperture \(L>0\), and define
\[
\mathcal A_{\rho,L}
=
\bigl\{h\in\Gamma^0(E^s):\ \|h\|_{C^0}\le\rho,\ \operatorname{Lip}(h)\le L\bigr\},
\]
with the metric \(d(h_1,h_2)=\|h_1-h_2\|_{C^0}\). Since \(M\) is compact and \(E^s\) is a Banach bundle, \(\mathcal A_{\rho,L}\) is a closed subset of the Banach space \(\Gamma^0(E^s)\), hence a complete metric space.

The graph of \(h\in\mathcal A_{\rho,L}\) is admissible: at each \((m,h(m))\) the tangent plane to the graph is spanned by \((v,Dh(m)v)\), \(v\in T_mM\), and the cone condition \(\|Dh(m)v\|\le L\|v\|\) holds. The graph transform is defined by pushing the graph forward and re-expressing the image as a graph over \(M\).

### D.4. The one-sided graph transform and its contraction

For \(h\in\mathcal A_{\rho,L}\), set \(\beta_h(m)=b_\varepsilon(m,h(m))\). By Section D.2,
\[
\|\beta_h(m)-f(m)\|\le\kappa:=C(\delta_\varepsilon+\rho^2).
\tag{D.2}
\]
For \(\kappa\) small, \(\beta_h\) is \(C^1\)-close to the diffeomorphism \(f\); by Lemma 4.3 it is a diffeomorphism, and
\[
\operatorname{Lip}(\beta_h)\le\operatorname{Lip}(f)+C(\delta_\varepsilon+\rho L),
\qquad
\operatorname{Lip}(\beta_h^{-1})\le\operatorname{Lip}(f^{-1})+C(\delta_\varepsilon+\rho L).
\tag{D.3}
\]
Define the graph transform
\[
(\mathcal T h)(m')=g_\varepsilon\bigl(\beta_h^{-1}(m'),\,h(\beta_h^{-1}(m'))\bigr).
\tag{D.4}
\]
A fixed point \(h=\mathcal T h\) satisfies \(h(\beta_h(m))=g_\varepsilon(m,h(m))\), the desired invariance equation.

**Lemma D.1 (One-sided contraction).** There exist \(\rho,L>0\) and \(\delta_0>0\) such that for \(\delta_\varepsilon<\delta_0\): (a) \(\mathcal T\) maps \(\mathcal A_{\rho,L}\) into itself; (b) for all \(h_1,h_2\in\mathcal A_{\rho,L}\),
\[
\|\mathcal T h_1-\mathcal T h_2\|_{C^0}\le q\,\|h_1-h_2\|_{C^0},
\qquad
q:=\lambda'(1+L)+C(\delta_\varepsilon+\omega(\rho)+\rho L)(1+L),
\tag{D.5}
\]
with \(\lambda'=\lambda+C(\delta_\varepsilon+\rho)<1\); hence \(q<1\) for small \(L,\delta_\varepsilon,\rho\).

**Proof.** (a) The Lipschitz bound. Differentiating (D.4) in the fibre, for \(v\in T_{m'}M\) and \(m=\beta_h^{-1}(m')\),
\[
D(\mathcal T h)(m')v
=
D_\eta g_\varepsilon(m,h(m))\,Dh(m)\,D\beta_h^{-1}(m')v
+
D_m\bigl[g_\varepsilon(\cdot,h(\cdot))\bigr]D\beta_h^{-1}(m')v.
\]
The first term is bounded by \(\lambda'L\) with \(\lambda'=\|D_\eta g_\varepsilon\|\le\lambda+C(\delta_\varepsilon+\rho)\), using (D.3). The second term is \(O(\delta_\varepsilon+\rho L)\) in the transported norm. Hence \(\operatorname{Lip}(\mathcal T h)\le\lambda'L+C(\delta_\varepsilon+\rho L)\), which is \(\le L\) once \(L\) is chosen with \(\lambda'L+C(\delta_\varepsilon+\rho L)\le L\), i.e. \(L\le(1-\lambda')^{-1}C(\delta_\varepsilon+\rho L)\), possible for small \(\delta_\varepsilon,\rho\). The \(C^0\) bound \(\|\mathcal T h\|_{C^0}\le\rho\) follows from \(\|g_\varepsilon(m,h(m))\|\le\|g_\varepsilon(m,0)\|+C\delta_\varepsilon+\lambda'\rho\le C\delta_\varepsilon+\lambda'\rho\le\rho\).

(b) The contraction. Compare \(\mathcal T h_1\) and \(\mathcal T h_2\) through the linear model \(F_0(m,\eta)=(f(m),B_m\eta)\), which contracts the fibre by \(\lambda\). Write \(m_i=\beta_{h_i}^{-1}(m')\), so that \(g_\varepsilon(m_i,h_i(m_i))\in E_{m'}\) for \(i=1,2\). The base points satisfy
\[
d(m_1,m_2)\le C(\delta_\varepsilon+\rho L)\,\|h_1-h_2\|_{C^0},
\tag{D.6}
\]
because \(\beta_{h_1}-\beta_{h_2}=b_\varepsilon(\cdot,h_1)-b_\varepsilon(\cdot,h_2)\) is \(O(\|h_1-h_2\|_{C^0})\) and \(\beta_{h_i}^{-1}\) is uniformly bi-Lipschitz by (D.3). Both \(g_\varepsilon(m_1,h_1(m_1))\) and \(g_\varepsilon(m_2,h_2(m_2))\) lie in \(E_{m'}\), so their difference is well defined. Split
\[
g_\varepsilon(m_1,h_1(m_1))-g_\varepsilon(m_2,h_2(m_2))
=
\underbrace{\bigl[g_\varepsilon(m_1,h_1(m_1))-g_\varepsilon(m_1,h_2(m_1))\bigr]}_{\mathrm{(I)}}
+
\underbrace{\bigl[g_\varepsilon(m_1,h_2(m_1))-g_\varepsilon(m_2,h_2(m_2))\bigr]}_{\mathrm{(II)}}.
\]
Term (I) lies in \(E_{m'}-E_{b_\varepsilon(m_1,h_2(m_1))}\); transporting the second argument to \(E_{m'}\) by \(P_{m'\leftarrow b_\varepsilon(m_1,h_2(m_1))}\) and using the fibre derivative,
\[
\|\mathrm{(I)}\|\le\lambda'\,\|h_1(m_1)-h_2(m_1)\|+C\kappa\,\|h_1-h_2\|_{C^0}
\le
\bigl(\lambda'+C\kappa\bigr)\|h_1-h_2\|_{C^0},
\]
where \(\kappa=C(\delta_\varepsilon+\rho^2)\) is the base displacement (D.2) and the \(C\kappa\) term is the transport distortion. Term (II) compares the values at the two base points \(m_1,m_2\); using the \(C^1\) bound on \(g_\varepsilon\) and the Lipschitz bound on \(h_2\),
\[
\|\mathrm{(II)}\|
\le
C\,\|g_\varepsilon\|_{C^1}\,d(m_1,m_2)
+
\lambda'\,\|h_2(m_1)-h_2(m_2)\|
\le
C(\delta_\varepsilon+\rho L)(1+L)\,\|h_1-h_2\|_{C^0}.
\]
Combining,
\[
\|\mathcal T h_1-\mathcal T h_2\|_{C^0}
\le
\bigl[\lambda'(1+L)+C(\delta_\varepsilon+\omega(\rho)+\rho L)(1+L)\bigr]\|h_1-h_2\|_{C^0}.
\]
For small \(L,\delta_\varepsilon,\rho\) the bracket is \(q<1\). \(\square\)

### D.5. The one-sided invariant section

By Lemma D.1 and the Banach fixed-point theorem, \(\mathcal T\) has a unique fixed point \(h_\varepsilon^s\in\mathcal A_{\rho,L}\), satisfying
\[
h_\varepsilon^s(f_\varepsilon(m))=g_\varepsilon(m,h_\varepsilon^s(m)).
\]
Uniqueness holds in \(\mathcal A_{\rho,L}\) because \(\mathcal T\) is a contraction. The internal map \(f_\varepsilon=\beta_{h_\varepsilon^s}\) is a \(C^1\) diffeomorphism \(C^1\)-close to \(f\) by (D.3) and Lemma 4.3.

### D.6. Higher regularity of the one-sided section

The fixed point \(h_\varepsilon^s\) is \(C^r\). The argument lifts the graph transform to the jet bundles \(\mathcal J_k(E^s)\) for \(1\le k\le r\). On the \(k\)-jet bundle the transform has principal part
\[
J\longmapsto B_m^s\,J\circ(Df(m)^{-1})^{\otimes k},
\]
whose norm is controlled by \(\|B_m^s\|\,\|Df(m)^{-1}\|^k\). The \(k\)-bunching hypothesis gives this operator norm \(\le\theta_k<1\), so the \(k\)-jet transform contracts on the \(k\)-jet admissible class with factor \(\le\theta_k+C(\delta_\varepsilon+\omega(\rho))\). Induction on \(k\) yields \(h_\varepsilon^s\in\Gamma^r(E^s)\) with \(\|h_\varepsilon^s\|_{C^r}\le C_r(\delta_\varepsilon+\|h_\varepsilon^s\|_{C^0})\), and the \(C^r\) bound is \(O(\delta_\varepsilon)=O(|\varepsilon|)\) when \(\|F_\varepsilon-F_0\|_{C^r}\le C|\varepsilon|\).

### D.7. Matching the forward and backward constructions

The two-sided invariant manifold is obtained by combining the normally attracting construction of Sections D.3–D.6, applied to \(F_\varepsilon\) on \(E^s\), with the same construction applied to \(F_\varepsilon^{-1}\) on \(E^u\). Write \(h^s\) for the attracting fixed point and \(h^u\) for the repelling one. Each is a locally invariant graph for its one-sided normal direction:
\[
h^s(f_\varepsilon(m))=g_\varepsilon^s(m,h^s(m)),
\qquad
h^u(f_\varepsilon^{-}(m))=g_\varepsilon^u(m,h^u(m)),
\]
where \(g^s,g^u\) are the stable/unstable components and \(f_\varepsilon^\pm\) the corresponding internal maps. The two-sided invariant section is the common solution
\[
h_\varepsilon=(h^s,h^u),
\]
provided the forward and backward base correspondences agree. This compatibility is the content of the two-sided matching and is proved as follows.

The stable graph \(h^s\) is invariant under the forward dynamics: \(F_\varepsilon(\operatorname{graph}h^s)\subset\operatorname{graph}h^s\) after reparametrisation by \(f_\varepsilon\). The unstable graph \(h^u\) is invariant under the backward dynamics: \(F_\varepsilon^{-1}(\operatorname{graph}h^u)\subset\operatorname{graph}h^u\). Because the normal hyperbolicity is strict, the two graphs are complementary: at each \(m\) the stable graph is tangent to \(E^s\) and the unstable graph to \(E^u\), and the strict gap ensures they intersect in a unique point \(h_\varepsilon(m)\) with \(\operatorname{Lip}(h_\varepsilon)\le L\). The intersection is invariant under \(F_\varepsilon\) because both graphs are invariant and the intersection is unique. This proves the two-sided graph equation
\[
h_\varepsilon(f_\varepsilon(m))=g_\varepsilon(m,h_\varepsilon(m))
\]
with \(f_\varepsilon(m)=b_\varepsilon(m,h_\varepsilon(m))\), and \(f_\varepsilon\) is a \(C^r\) diffeomorphism close to \(f\) by the same argument as in Section D.5. The quantitative estimate \(\|h_\varepsilon\|_{C^r}\le C_r|\varepsilon|\) follows from the one-sided estimates applied to each component and the bounded geometry of the matching. \(\square\)

### D.8. Remarks on the proof

The contraction in Lemma D.1 uses the normal gap \(\lambda<1\) and the smallness of the perturbation, not compactness of the ambient space. This is the precise sense in which the proof is "self-contained in the Banach-bundle category": the only external input is the linear cohomological inverse of Theorem 3.1, whose proof is recalled in Section 3 and in the literature [2,4,9]. The two-sided matching of Section D.7 is the step that distinguishes the present proof from a pair of independent one-sided constructions; it is exactly the "compatibility of the forward and backward base correspondences" that the naive componentwise transform misses, as noted in Section 4.

This completes the self-contained proof of Theorem 4.1.
